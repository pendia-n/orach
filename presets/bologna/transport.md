The system works in three ways.\n\nWith the **Roger** app (iOS and Android, free) you buy a digital ticket and validate it by holding your phone against the green validator on board. It also works offline once purchased.\n\n**Tobacconists and newsagents** sell QR-code paper tickets. In the centre you’ll find one every few hundred metres; sales points are also shown in the app.\n\nWith a **contactless bank card or NFC phone** you tap the green validator on the bus directly, with no pre-purchase needed. The fare is the same as a standard ticket. Paying with coins is possible on some lines, but costs more.\n\n## Single ticket, daily pass or contactless: what’s best\n\n| Ticket type | Price | Validity | When to use it |\n| --- | --- | --- | --- |\n| Single ticket | €2.30 | 75 minutes | 1–3 trips per day |\n| 10-trip carnet | €19 (€1.90/trip) | 75 min per trip | Multi-day stays |\n| 24-hour pass | €9 | 24 hours unlimited | 4 or more trips on the same day |\n| Contactless (card/phone) | Same as single, automatic capping | Per trip | Those who want to hop on without thinking |\n\nWith contactless, every time you use the same card the system applies the cheapest available fare. If you make enough trips within 24 hours, you won’t pay more than a day pass — without having to buy one in advance.\n\nThe 24-hour pass pays off from four trips upward: 4 × €2.30 = €9.20. If you already know you’ll be on the move a lot, buy it in the morning.\n\n## When to take the bus — and when not to\n\nThe arcades cover nearly 40 km in the historic centre alone.
