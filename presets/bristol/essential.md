They are generally found only in urban and suburban areas, where they have been common features since the 1960s.\n\nFrom 1985 to 2010, the number of launderettes has declined, with only around 3000 remaining.[[13]](#cite_note-khan20100813-13) [[14]](#cite_note-14)Rapidly rising utility charges, premises rent and a lower purchase cost of domestic machines have been noted as principal reasons for the recent decline. High initial launch costs, specifically for commercial washing machines and dryers, have also been commented on as reasons for fewer new entrants into the market. Furthermore, machine updates can be prohibitively expensive, which has held back premises' investment.\n\n[![](//thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/7/75/Alfresco_launderette%2C_Hull_-_geograph.org.uk_-_6884968.jpg/250px-Alfresco_launderette%2C_Hull_-_geograph.org.uk_-_6884968.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Alfresco_launderette,_Hull_-_geograph.org.uk_-_6884968.jpg)\n\nAn open air launderette in Hull\n\nMany of the staffed operations in the UK have added-value services such as ironing, dry cleaning and service washes, which are popular among professionals, students, and senior citizens. Student accommodation blocks often have their own unstaffed laundries, which are typically run at a profit by the accommodation provider.\n\nLocal phone directories only show laundries that pay to be included, so trends are difficult to assess.[*[original research?](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:No_original_research \"Wikipedia:No original research\")*] However, large cities such as Birmingham, Bristol, Leeds, Liverpool, London, Manchester, Sheffield and Southampton have significant numbers of launderettes, as do many coastal tourist areas.\n\nThe main manufacturers serving the UK in this market are Electrolux, IPSO, Maytag, and Primus. Brands such as Frigidaire and [Speed Queen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Speed_Queen \"Speed Queen\") are also regularly deployed, with most originating from Belgium and the US.\n\n### United States\n\n[[edit](/w/index.php?title=Self-service_laundry&action=edit&section=11 \"Edit section: United States\")]\n\n[![](//thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/1/1b/5029_Germantown.JPG/250px-5029_Germantown.JPG?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:5029_Germantown.JPG)\n\nLaundromat in [Philadelphia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philadelphia \"Philadelphia\") that opened in 1947 and is believed to be the first coin-operated laundromat in the [United States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States \"United States\")\n\n [![](//thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/3/33/Lmaries.jpg/250px-Lmaries.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Lmaries.jpg)\n\nA modern laundromat in [Ohio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ohio \"Ohio\")\n\nSelf-service laundry facilities in the United States are most commonly called laundromats. The term \"laundromat\" is the [genericized trademark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Genericized_trademark \"Genericized trademark\") of the [Westinghouse Electric Corporation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Westinghouse_Electric_(1886) \"Westinghouse Electric (1886)\"), later [White Consolidated Industries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/White_Consolidated_Industries \"White Consolidated Industries\"),[[15]](#cite_note-uspto-15) and was created by its employee, [George Edward Pendray](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Edward_Pendray \"George Edward Pendray\").[[16]](#cite_note-brief-16)[[17]](#cite_note-Laundromat-17) \"Washateria\" is an alternate name for laundromat, but is not in common use outside of Texas. The term comes from the first laundromat in the United States, which was known as a *washateria* and was opened on 18 April 1934 in [Fort Worth, Texas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fort_Worth,_Texas \"Fort Worth, Texas\"), by C.A. Tannahill.[[18]](#cite_note-18) Though steam-powered laundry machines were invented in the 19th century, their cost put them out of reach of many. Cantrell and others began renting short-term use of their machines.[[19]](#cite_note-D'Costa-19) Most laundromats in the US are fully automated and [coin-operated](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coin-operated \"Coin-operated\") and generally unstaffed, with many operating 24 hours a day.[[20]](#cite_note-20) The invention of the coin-operated laundry machine is ascribed to Harry Greenwald of New York who created Greenwald Industries in 1957; the company marketed the devices through the 20th century.[[21]](#cite_note-21)[[22]](#cite_note-22)[[23]](#cite_note-23) While coin laundromats are very common, some laundromats accept credit cards or provide their own card system.\n\nThe United States Census Bureau estimates that there are 11,000 of this style of laundromat in the US, employing 39,000 people and generating over $3.4 billion every year.[[24]](#cite_note-US_Census-24)\n\n## In popular culture\n\n[[edit](/w/index.php?title=Self-service_laundry&action=edit&section=12 \"Edit section: In popular culture\")]\n\n* *[My Beautiful Laundrette](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/My_Beautiful_Laundrette \"My Beautiful Laundrette\"),* 1985 film\n* *[My Beautiful Laundrette](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/My_Beautiful_Laundrette_(play) \"My Beautiful Laundrette (play)\")*, 2019 play adapted from the above film and written by the same individual who wrote the film's screenplay ([Hanif Kureishi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hanif_Kureishi \"Hanif Kureishi\"))\n\n## See also\n\n[[edit](/w/index.php?title=Self-service_laundry&action=edit&section=13 \"Edit section: See also\")]\n\n* [Laundress](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laundress \"Laundress\") or washerwoman, who used to do similar work\n* [Mangle (machine)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mangle_(machine) \"Mangle (machine)\")\n* [Shared laundry rooms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laundry#Shared_laundry_rooms \"Laundry\")\n\n## References\n\n[[edit](/w/index.php?title=Self-service_laundry&action=edit&section=14 \"Edit section: References\")]\n\n1.
