It’s been served at traditional Korean restaurants for centuries; cafés have adopted it as a cold drink that’s non-caffeinated and very refreshing in summer. Served in tall glasses, often on ice. Tart, refreshing, and very good in summer. Not technically coffee, but ubiquitous at Korean cafés as a non-caffeinated option.\n\n**Traditional teas:** Many cafés in tourist areas and older neighborhoods serve a range of Korean herbal teas — omija (five-flavor berry), ssanghwa-tang (a warming herbal blend), chrysanthemum tea — either as a standalone menu or as part of a traditional tea house format. You order and pay first, receive a table number or buzzer, and your drink is brought to you or you pick it up when your name or number is called. Sitting at a table and waiting for someone to come to you will result in you sitting indefinitely.\n\n**Minimum order:** Some cafés (particularly those with prime view seating) have a minimum order per person. This is usually one drink and will be posted near the ordering counter. The minimum is non-negotiable.\n\n**No tipping:** Tipping is not customary in South Korea. Pay the listed price for your drinks, nothing more. This applies at every café format from the most casual to the most serious specialty shop.\n\n**Lingering is normal:** As noted above, Korean café culture involves extended stays. A two-hour visit with one drink is completely normal. Nobody will indicate you should leave or give you a look. You don’t need to buy a second drink to justify occupying your seat. This is why cafés are often crowded at peak times — the turnover is slow by design.\n\n**Noise levels vary:** Some Korean cafés play music at a level that makes conversation difficult; others are quiet. This varies by establishment and is not easily predicted from the outside. If you’re looking for a quiet work or reading café, look for spots that describe themselves as “작업실” (work room) format — these tend toward lower noise and longer stays.\n\n## Price reference across café types\n\nCafé food (desserts, snacks, light meals): 6,000–15,000 KRW ($4.50–11). While Thursday Party offers a wide selection of beers and a chance to mingle, Hit The Beach presents breathtaking views with a predominantly Korean crowd.\n\n[![night views at Beacon Fire Station on Hwangryeong Mountain in Busan, South Korea](https://live.staticflickr.com/65535/53543586812_e9884026fe_z.jpg)](https://live.staticflickr.com/65535/53543586812_e20f1f891f_h.jpg \"night views at Beacon Fire Station on Hwangryeong Mountain\")night views at Beacon Fire Station on Hwangryeong Mountain\n\nThe most incredible views can be enjoyed at the Beacon Fire Station on **Hwangryeong Mountain**, which locals like to refer to as Pallam Mountain. The observatory deck is an excellent date spot that provides 360-degree views of Busan, Gwangan Bridge and Kyungsung. The best time to go is during sunset, and I suggest you take a taxi.\n\nGwangalli Beach may not be as vibrant as Seomyeon but makes up for it with its many date spots, from **Hotel 1 Rooftop** to **Cafe Haute** which offer very date-friendly booths and views of Gwangan Bridge often seen in Korean drama shows. At night time you can grab drinks here at **Thursday Party Gwangalli** or Cocky Pub.\n\n[![Pig Village - pork BBQ heaven in Busan, South Korea](https://live.staticflickr.com/65535/53543586772_77ca3ca3e4_z.jpg)](https://live.staticflickr.com/65535/53543586772_1b3c173aa5_h.jpg \"Pig Village - pork BBQ heaven\")Pig Village - pork BBQ heaven\n\nFor techno enthusiasts, **Output** is the go-to spot, with an affordable cover charge and the flexibility of re-entry, making it perfect for those who wish to explore multiple venues in one night. Meanwhile, **Fuzzy Navel** and **Cream Nightclub** cater to those seeking a diverse night out with options ranging from laid-back bars to vibrant hip-hop scenes.\n\nBusan has a bigger food scene than a nightlife scene. You'll find a plethora of restaurants on every street. Our favorite restaurants included Yaki-ya, Pig Village and Joseon Kalguksu (which is open 24/7). For more great cafes, restaurants, bars and nightclubs make sure to download my **Busan Master Map** listed above.\n\n[![Haeundae Traditional Market in Busan, South Korea](https://live.staticflickr.com/65535/53544641213_c09340cc9a_z.jpg)](https://live.staticflickr.com/65535/53544641213_8420acfb37_h.jpg \"Haeundae Traditional Market\")Haeundae Traditional Market\n\n**Haeundae Beach**, the upscale district of Busan. Here you can often expect a sand castle competition on the beach. You can also find hardcore local cuisine in the **Haeundae Market Street**. For nightlife, hit up **Thursday Party Haeundae** or **Dormer** and **TBR (The Back Room)** for cocktails and a more relaxed atmosphere. For the best views in Haeundae Beach head over to **Busan X The Sky**.\n\n**Kyungsung**, the student district, offers a more local atmosphere with authentic bars and restaurants, perfect for those looking to experience the real Busan. **Gwangalli Beach** and **Haeundae Beach** add romantic and upscale options to the mix, from breathtaking rooftop views at **Hotel 1 Rooftop** to unique cocktail experiences at **Muse-on**. Another good date spot is [**Deoksu's Dog Cafe**](https://map.naver.com/p/entry/place/37774786?c=20.00,0,0,0,dh), fun doggy cafe in Kyungsung.\n\n[![Deoksu's Dog Cafe in Busan, South Korea](https://live.staticflickr.com/65535/53544892995_cd54307c8f_z.jpg)](https://live.staticflickr.com/65535/53544892995_a4af9f45a5_h.jpg \"Deoksu's Dog Cafe\")Deoksu's Dog Cafe\n\nIn conclusion, Busan's nightlife is a rich tapestry of experiences, from lively bars and clubs in Seomyeon to romantic beachfront venues in Gwangalli and Haeundae. Whether you're looking to party until dawn, enjoy a quiet cocktail with stunning views, or explore local flavors in Kyungsung, Busan has something for every night owl. With its diverse offerings, vibrant atmosphere, and welcoming locals, Busan is truly a champion of the night, promising unforgettable experiences for visitors and residents alike.\n\n[Load more
