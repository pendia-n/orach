Round it out with **baharat** (a warm all-purpose blend), cinnamon, cardamom and dried mint, and you’ve got the toolkit behind most Cairo cooking.\n\nIf you want to take the flavours home, the spice stalls of Islamic Cairo around Khan el-Khalili are the place — heaped, fragrant, and far cheaper than a supermarket. Picture rows of cane chairs spilling onto the pavement, men slapping down dominoes and backgammon, the bubble of shisha pipes, mint tea in little glasses, and absolutely no rush to leave. **Fishawi’s** in Khan el-Khalili has been doing this for over two centuries and is the obvious first one; touristy, yes, but the setting earns it.\n\nOrder coffee the local way: it’s like Turkish coffee, ground fine and brewed in a little copper *kanaka*, thick with grounds at the bottom. A coffee or tea at an ahwa is cheap, often 20-50 EGP; a shisha adds maybe 50-100. Then there are the juice stands, which are a genuine Cairo institution: a glass of fresh-pressed mango, guava, sugarcane, orange, strawberry or pomegranate for 20-50 EGP off practically any corner. In summer a cold sugarcane (*asab*) or a thick mango shake fixes the heat instantly. In winter, swap to **sahlab**, a warm milky orchid-root drink dusted with nuts and cinnamon.\n\nA couple more worth knowing: **sobia**, a sweet coconut-milk drink that surges during Ramadan, and **‘erk sous**, a dark, intensely bitter-sweet liquorice-root drink poured by sash-wearing vendors who clack brass cups to announce themselves — try a small one for the experience even if you don’t finish it. We asked four Cairo locals and food lovers to share their must-eat places across the city—from gritty street-side joints to modern institutions.
