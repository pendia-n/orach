For every kind of Biltong — from beef to springbok and ostrich – as well as special Biltong knives and choppers, visit a dedicated shop like Joubert & Monty at the V&A Waterfront.\n\n![Image 23: A plate of nine oval-shaped, golden-brown, coconut-coated pastries.](https://cdn.getyourguide.com/image/format=auto%2Cfit=cover%2Cgravity=auto%2Cquality=60/dam/t187932.-Cape-Town---3-Hour-Malay-Cooking-Class-Lunch-in-Bo-Kaap-nr6.LOWRES.jpg)\n\nphoto: Bernelee Kritzinger\n\nSweat treats don’t get much better than this traditional South African sticky donut.\n\n## 8. Koesisters\n\nA popular sweet treat in Cape Town, koesisters are traditional Cape Malay spiced donuts. Perfect for [Christmas in Cape Town](https://www.getyourguide.com/explorer/cape-town-ttd103/christmas-in-capetown/), these deep-fried dough balls are flavored with festive spices such as cinnamon, cardamom, and ginger, and then coated in a sticky, sweet syrup.\n\n### How to eat the best koesisters in Cape Town?\n\nYou can find freshly made koesisters in bakeries across the city. Malva pudding\n\nWhile the origin of malva pudding and its name may cause some debate — is it Dutch or not? Waterblommetjie bredie\n\nAs its name suggests — “stew of small water flowers” — this traditional Cape Malay dish is a slow-cooked lamb stew with a healthy helping of vegetables, including onions and potatoes. The crowning glory is _waterblommetjie_, which means “water flower,” a local aquatic plant endemic to the Western Cape. It has a slightly bitter, earthy flavor that balances the stew’s richness.\n\n### How to eat the best waterblommetjie bredie in Cape Town?\n\nWaterblommetjies only grow in the wet, winter months, meaning this Western Cape delicacy is best enjoyed from July to September. For a stew with a view, try it at Karibu Restaurant at the [V&A Waterfront](https://www.getyourguide.co.uk/va-waterfront-l3861/).\n\n## FAQs\n\n### What is the best time of year for food lovers to visit Cape Town?\n\nSpring and [summer in Cape Town](https://www.getyourguide.com/explorer/cape-town-ttd103/capetown-in-summer/) (September to April) are popular with foodies. The weather is warm and sunny, so many restaurants offer outdoor seating. This is also the peak season for fresh produce, so you’ll find an abundance of delicious fruit and vegetables at local markets.\n\n### Where can I find the best street food to eat in Cape Town?\n\nCape Town loves its street food. Try koesisters and _samoosas_ (fried pastry snacks) in the Bo-Kaap neighborhood, or grab fish and chips at the V&A Waterfront. For an authentic South African braai (barbecue), head to Mzoli’s in [Gugulethu](https://www.getyourguide.co.uk/gugulethu-l133147/) and pick your own meat.\n\n### What is a good budget for food in Cape Town?\n\nYou can expect to spend around ZAR200-300 ($10-15) daily on food in Cape Town if you’re eating at mid-range restaurants. On a tight budget, you can easily find street food for under ZAR100 ($5).\n\n### Are there vegetarian options readily available?\n\nWhile South Africa is known for its love of meat and barbecue, you’ll find vegetarian curries and other meat-free meals on most menus. Popular vegetarian and vegan restaurants in Cape Town include The Conscious Kitchen and Addis in Cape.\n\n### What is the dining etiquette in Cape Town?\n\nDining etiquette in Cape Town is generally relaxed and informal.
