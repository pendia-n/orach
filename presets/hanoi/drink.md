Alcoholic beverages\n\n**Beer:**\n\n![Saigon beer can standing out against the green and red cans](https://www.vietnamdrive.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/12/saigon-beer-vietnam.jpg)\n\nSaigon beer is one of the most widely consumed alcoholic drinks in Vietnam.\n\nBeer is the most popular alcoholic beverage in Vietnam, with consumption among the highest in the region (Vietnam consumed 4,400 million liters in 2024). In Hanoi, traditional draft beer shops still exist, while bottled and canned beers from various domestic brands are widely available in both urban and rural areas.\n\n**Traditional rice wine:**  \nRice wine is a long-standing Vietnamese alcoholic drink, originating from wet-rice culture and distilled from white rice or sticky rice with natural yeast. It typically has a high alcohol content and a strong flavor, with sticky rice wine being more fragrant and easier to drink. Each region has its own variation, such as rice wine in the South, the famous Bau Da wine from Binh Dinh, or can wine in the Central Highlands, which is consumed in a traditional communal style. Today, tourists can find rice wine at festivals, specialty restaurants, or when visiting rural areas.\n\n**Dalat wine & grape wine:**  \nDalat wine is the most popular wine in Vietnam and is widely available in restaurants serving tourists. With a light alcohol content of about 11–14% ABV and an easy-to-drink taste, it is a familiar choice for dinners and formal occasions. In addition to bottled wine, grape-growing regions in Ninh Thuan and Binh Thuan also produce naturally fermented grape wine with a rich sweetness. This type of wine can be enjoyed straight or diluted as a soft drink, making it a distinctive local beverage from this sunny, sandy region.\n\n**Modern Vietnamese cocktails:**  \nThis category has been growing in popularity in major cities such as Hanoi, Da Nang, and Ho Chi Minh City. Although not as common as beer or rice wine, many high-end bars and restaurants have created cocktails with a Vietnamese twist by incorporating local ingredients such as lemongrass, ginger, passion fruit, or sticky rice wine. How to Order Drinks in Vietnam (international visitors may need to use)\n\nA type of drink in Vietnam often has many different variations. For example, coffee has **cà phê đen** (black coffee), **cà phê sữa** (milk coffee – also called **nu**), **cà phê nóng** (hot coffee), and **cà phê đá** (iced coffee); tea has **trà gừng nóng** (hot ginger tea) or **trà gừng lạnh** (cold ginger tea). Therefore, to avoid confusion when ordering, especially in places where there are no staff to guide you in detail, you can refer to the following notes.\n\n### How to classify drinks when ordering:\n\n* **Hot / cold (Nóng / lạnh)**: drinks served hot or with ice.\n* **Level of ice**: little ice or a lot of ice (**ít đá** or **nhiều đá**).\n* **Sweetness**: with sugar (**có đường**) or no sugar (**không đường**); little sugar (**ít đường**) or a lot of sugar (**nhiều đường**).\n* **Type of milk**: condensed milk (**sữa đặc**) or fresh milk (**sữa tươi**).  \n  **Note:** Vietnamese coffee defaults to condensed milk when you order “milk coffee” (**cà phê sữa**).\n\n### How to order popular types of coffee:\n\n* **Đen đá** (black ice): black coffee with sugar, served with ice.\n* **Nu đá** (brown ice): iced milk coffee (coffee + condensed milk + ice).\n* **Đen nóng** (black hot): black coffee with sugar, served hot (no ice).\n* **Bạc xỉu**: more condensed milk and very little coffee; sweeter and lighter than regular iced milk coffee (**cà phê sữa đá**).\n\n### Common English words understood in Vietnamese restaurants:\n\nMenu, order, and bill are widely understood in cafés and restaurants serving tourists.  \nIf you want to compliment a drink or any dish of [Vietnamese food](https://www.vietnamdrive.com/travel-guide/vietnamese-food/), you can use simple Vietnamese words like **Ngon** (delicious) or **Tuyệt** (great).\n\n### Common mistakes tourists make when ordering drinks, and how to say it correctly:\n\n**1. “Sugarcane juice with lime” – Wrong**\n\n* In Vietnam, Sugarcane juice (**nước mía**) is usually mixed with **tắc** (kumquat), not lime.\n* **Correct:** Sugarcane juice with kumquat (**nước mía tắc**).\n\n**2. “Trà chanh không đường” (lemon tea, no sugar) – misleading**\n\n* The seller may still serve sweet **trà chanh**, because the powdered mix already contains sugar.\n* **Better to ask:** “**Bạn có trà chanh không ngọt không?**” to confirm its taste.\n* **Correct:** **Trà chanh không ngọt** (lemon tea, no sweet).\n\n**3. “Hot tea” – often brings Lipton**\n\nAt modern cafés, ordering hot tea usually results in **Lipton nóng** (hot Lipton tea), as it is the default.\n\nTo drink Vietnamese tea, ask for:\n\n* **Trà xanh nóng** (hot green tea)\n* **Trà hoa nhài nóng** (hot jasmine tea – common in Vietnam)\n* Or clearly say: “**Cho tôi bình trà Việt nóng.**” (May I have a hot Vietnamese tea pot?)\n\n![Vietnamese hot tea served in a pot with cups](https://www.vietnamdrive.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/12/hot-vietnamese-tea.jpg)\n\nA pot of Vietnamese hot tea (trà Việt nóng), which is different from Lipton tea commonly served when ordering “hot tea” at modern cafés.\n\n**4. Wrong way to order when wanting Bạc xỉu**\n\n* Saying “coffee with lots of milk” might get you **cà phê sữa đá** (milk coffee), not **bạc xỉu**.\n* **Correct:** Order **bạc xỉu** — a Saigon-style drink with little coffee and lots of milk, often served with ice. It’s one of those Vietnamese drinks, like **bạc xỉu**, that doesn’t have an exact translation.\n\n### Key Information Summary for Visitors\n\nThis table provides quick translations to help visitors order drinks and choose from their various versions.\n\n| Vietnamese | English |\n| Nóng | Hot |\n| Lạnh | Cold |\n| Ít đá | Little ice |\n| Nhiều đá | A lot of ice |\n| Ít đường | Little sugar |\n| Nhiều đường | A lot of sugar |\n| Không đường | No sugar / Unsweetened |\n| Sữa đặc | Condensed milk |\n| Sữa tươi | Fresh milk |\n| Cà phê đen | Black coffee |\n| Cà phê nu | Brown coffee |\n| Đen đá | Black coffee with ice |\n| Nu đá | Iced milk coffee |\n| Bạc xỉu | Bac xiu (little coffee, lots of milk) |\n\n## 6. Safety Tips When Drinking Beverages in Vietnam\n\n**Tips when using ice**  \nMost ice in small shops is made from regular filtered water, similar to [tap water](https://www.vietnamdrive.com/water-in-vietnam/). If you have a sensitive digestive system, be cautious when consuming iced drinks. A helpful tip is to buy a bottle of frozen water and use it in place of ice when needed.\n\n**Do not drink alcohol of unknown origin**  \nAvoid trying homemade or unlabeled alcohol, as the risk of contamination or poisoning is fairly high. Tourists should only drink alcohol at reputable restaurants or shops.\n\n**Choose a clean sugarcane juice stall**  \nSugarcane juice is extremely popular, but not all vendors ensure proper hygiene. The sugarcane may not be washed thoroughly, or the ice used may not be clean. For safer options, choose a stall with a clean juicer or buy sugarcane juice in supermarkets or commercial areas.\n\n**Be aware of fruit allergies**  \nSome people may be allergic to tropical fruits such as durian, avocado, or star apple. If you have never tried a particular fruit before, start with a small amount to avoid potential allergic reactions.\n\n**Choose reputable bottled water brands**  \nPrioritize well-known brands such as Aquafina or Dasani, and check that the bottle cap is tightly sealed. If you come across bottled water with packaging, color, or design similar to major brands but with unclear origins, be cautious.\n\n**Finish cold beverages early**  \nWhen drinking iced beverages, consume them before the ice melts completely. As the ice melts in hot weather, the drink’s temperature may rise to a level that encourages bacterial growth.\n\n**Do not add ice to boiling water**  \nAvoid dropping individual ice cubes into boiling water that is cooling down. During the warm stage, the mixture can create an ideal environment for bacteria to multiply rapidly.\n\n## 8. All of them are the \"wish I'd known\" variety.\n\n1. **Judging by a tourist-strip café.** A cà phê sữa đá next to a major sight is sugar with ice.
