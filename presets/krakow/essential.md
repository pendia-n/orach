Its exchange rate against the euro has been fairly stable for years, and inflation has gradually cooled in 2025-2026 after the 2022-2023 spike. Prices for goods sold by international retail chains generally track region-wide pricing rather than day-to-day currency fluctuations.\n\n## Public transport\n\nIn Warsaw, a single ticket (valid for 75 minutes on any mode of transport within a zone) costs about **PLN 4.60**. A monthly pass for the whole city transport network costs about **PLN 180**, noticeably more than a few years ago, but still cheaper than running a car in the city.\n\nOther major cities are comparable: Kraków, roughly PLN 4-4.50 for a single ticket and PLN 100-110 for a monthly pass; Wrocław and Gdańsk are at a similar level.\n\n## Fuel\n\nAs of mid-2026, average pump prices are:\n\n| Fuel type | Price per liter |\n| --- | --- |\n| 95 petrol | ≈ PLN 6.20 |\n| Diesel (ON) | ≈ PLN 6.90 |\n| LPG (autogas) | ≈ PLN 3.80 |\n\nPrices have risen noticeably compared to previous years, partly because several government anti-crisis fuel measures expired in the summer of 2026.\n\n## Groceries\n\nApproximate prices at major supermarket chains (2026):\n\n| Item | Price |\n| --- | --- |\n| Bread (~500 g loaf) | PLN 5-6 |\n| Milk (1 L) | PLN 4-5 |\n| Butter (200-250 g) | PLN 8-12 |\n| Local cheese (1 kg) | PLN 30-35 |\n| Eggs (10 pcs) | PLN 9-13 |\n| Tomatoes (1 kg) | PLN 8-11 |\n| Coca-Cola (2 L) | PLN 10-12 |\n| Chocolate bar (~100 g) | PLN 4-6 |\n\nAt small neighborhood shops (*sklepy osiedlowe*), farmers’ markets (*targi*), and gas-station shops, the same items typically cost **20-40% more** than at large supermarket chains; that gap hasn’t changed much over the years.\n\n## Alcohol and cigarettes\n\nAlcohol and tobacco carry high excise duties in Poland, so prices are noticeably higher than in Ukraine, Belarus, or Russia:\n\n| Item | Price |\n| --- | --- |\n| Vodka (0.5 L) | PLN 25-35 |\n| Local beer (0.5 L, store) | PLN 4.50-5.50 |\n| Mid-range wine (0.75 L) | PLN 25-30 |\n| Cigarettes (pack, mid-range) | ≈ PLN 18-20 |\n\n## Entertainment and services\n\n* **Cinema**: averages PLN 25-30 per ticket; up to PLN 40-45 in big multiplexes on weekends. Students typically get about a 50% discount.\n* **Gym membership**: basic plans run PLN 120-150/month, premium chains PLN 180-220/month.\n* **Coffee (latte/cappuccino) at a chain café**: PLN 10-15.\n* **Bank account with a debit card**: usually free if you use it actively.\n\n## Rent\n\nRenting a studio or 1-bedroom apartment in a city center in 2026 costs roughly:\n\n| City | Monthly rent |\n| --- | --- |\n| Warsaw (city center) | PLN 4,000-5,500 |\n| Kraków (city center) | PLN 3,500-4,800 |\n| Wrocław (city center) | PLN 3,500-4,800 |\n\nPrices depend on the neighborhood, the building’s legal form (condominium, *Wspólnota Mieszkaniowa*, or housing cooperative, *Spółdzielnia Mieszkaniowa*), and whether perpetual usufruct land fees (*użytkowanie wieczyste*) apply.\n\nIf you’re running a B2B/freelance business and pricing your services for Polish clients, keep these living costs in mind alongside standard [ZUS](https://mojafirma.org/what-is-zus-in-poland/) contributions and the 2026 minimum-wage benchmark, especially if you’re structuring rates for a genuinely independent B2B arrangement that needs to withstand scrutiny under the 2026 PIP misclassification reform [here](https://mojafirma.org/pip-2026-b2b-poland-guide/).\n\n## FAQ\n\n**How much have prices in Poland risen in recent years?** Fuel, rent, and transport passes have risen the most, some by 1.5-2x compared to 2016-2017 levels.
