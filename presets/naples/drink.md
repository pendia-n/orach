per favore*”, which translates as “A coffee, please.”\n\nYou can swap “*un caffè*” for any of the coffees listed above.\n\n[READ NEXT\n\n25 Terrific Things to Do in Tuscany (+ Travel please” in Italian… but that’s really way too formal for most bars and unnecessary.\n\nWe highly recommend trying to get comfortable ordering coffee in Italian during your trip.\n\nIt’s fairly simple to do, the bartenders are generally very nice about it, and since it’s something you’ll (probably) do repeatedly during your trip, you’ll be able to see your language skills improve in real time each day.\n\nA few more Italian words and phrases you might like to use when ordering coffee in Italy include:\n\n***Buongiorno.*** — Good day.\n\n***Buonasera.*** — Good evening.\n\nIt’s always You’ll generally find *brioche* used in the northern reaches of Italy.\n\nYou’ll also often see more specific regional pastries available, but these simple options are available across the country!\n\n## **Unspoken Rules for Navigating Italian Coffee Culture**\n\n### **One to 1.5 Euro is a solid going price for a *caffè*.**\n\nThat is speaking very, very generally of course, but it’s a good rule of thumb. \n\nLike in much of the world, coffee prices in Italy have risen sharply over the last few years.\n\n[READ NEXT\n\n17 Wildly Romantic Places in Italy](https://www.ourescapeclause.com/most-romantic-places-in-italy/)\n\nWhen I first published this blog post, this tip referenced 1 Euro as being a solid going price, but **[1.16 Euro is now said to be the national average](https://www.nytimes.com/2025/07/12/world/europe/espresso-italy-coffee-prices.html)**.\n\nPractically, it varies dramatically by region and where you are (a rural town in Basilicata and a bar within steps of the Colosseum are wildly different places).\n\nHowever, if you ever catch yourself paying close to 1.5 Euro per *caffè*, you should consider moving on. is never expected for coffee at a bar.\n\nIf you feel like you received truly exceptional service, you can choose to round up the bill.\n\n### **Don’t specify the size of your coffee.**\n\nYou’re not ordering a small, medium, or large *caffè*: you’re ordering a *caffè*, and it will come in a designated *caffè* cup. \n\nThe size of your coffee in Italy is generally dictated by the kind of coffee you order, not the amount you would like.\n\n### **Italian bars must display their menu with prices.**\n\nThis isn’t always in the most obvious spot, but if you look closely, you’ll always be able to
