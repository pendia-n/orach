flora](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Australian_flora \"Australian flora\") and [fauna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Australian_fauna \"Australian fauna\") of the [Australian bush](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Australian_bush \"Australian bush\") for over 60,000 years.[[5]](#cite_note-5) In modern times, this collection of foods and customs has become known as bush tucker.[[6]](#cite_note-6) It is understood that Indigenous Australians ate up to 5,000 species of Australian flora and fauna.[[7]](#cite_note-7) Hunting of [kangaroo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kangaroo \"Kangaroo\"), [wallaby](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wallaby \"Wallaby\") and [emu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emu \"Emu\") was common,[[8]](#cite_note-8) with other foods widely consumed including [bogong moths](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bogong_moth \"Bogong moth\"), [witchetty grubs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Witchetty_grub \"Witchetty grub\"), lizards and snakes.[[9]](#cite_note-9) Bush berries, fruits, and nuts were also used, including the now widely cultivated [macadamia nut](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Macadamia_nut \"Macadamia nut\"), and wild honeys were also exploited.[[1]](#cite_note-cultureandrecreation.gov.au-1) Fish were caught using tools such as spears, hooks, and traps; in some areas, the construction of complex weir systems allowed the development of forms of aquaculture.[[10]](#cite_note-10)\n\nResource availability and dietary composition varied across regions, and scientific theories that bush tucker plants were spread by hand have recently emerged.[[11]](#cite_note-11) Food preparation techniques also varied; however, a common cooking technique was for the carcass to be thrown directly on a campfire to be roasted.[[12]](#cite_note-12) Native food sources were used to supplement the colonists' diet following the arrival of the [First Fleet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_Fleet \"First Fleet\") in [Botany Bay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Botany_Bay \"Botany Bay\") in 1788.[[13]](#cite_note-13)[[14]](#cite_note-14)\n\n![Bush tucker Development of Australian cuisine\n\n![](//thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/6/60/Tea_and_damper.jpg/250px-Tea_and_damper.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)\n\nFollowing the of Australia (1788–1850)\") began arriving with the First Fleet at Sydney Harbour in 1788.[[15]](#cite_note-15) The diet consisted of \"bread, [salted meat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salted_meat \"Salted meat\") and tea with lashings of rum (initially from the [West Indies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Indies \"West Indies\") but later made from the waste cane of the [sugar market. Queensland and [New South Wales](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_South_Wales \"New South Wales\") became Australia's main beef cattle producers, while dairy cattle farming is found in the southern states, predominantly in Victoria. Wheat and other grain crops are spread fairly evenly throughout the mainland states. [Sugar cane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sugar_cane \"Sugar cane\") is also a major crop in Queensland and New South Wales. Fruit and vegetables are grown throughout Australia[[18]](#cite_note-18), and wheat is a main component of the Australian diet.[[19]](#cite_note-bulletin-19) Today there are over 85,681 farm businesses in Australia, 99 percent of which are locally owned and operated.[[20]](#cite_note-20) Barbecued meat is almost synonymous with Australian cuisine, though it is estimated that more than 10% of Australians are now vegetarian.[[21]](#cite_note-21)[[22]](#cite_note-22)\n\n### Modern Australian cuisine\n\n![](//thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/b/b6/Tetsuyas-Ocean-Trout.jpg/330px-Tetsuyas-Ocean-Trout.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)\n\nAfter [World War II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_War_II \"World War II\"), subsequent waves of [multicultural](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multicultural \"Multicultural\") immigration, with a majority drawn from Asia and the Mediterranean region, and the strong, sophisticated food cultures these ethnic communities have brought with them, influenced the development of Australian cuisine. This blending of \"European techniques and Asian flavours\" came to be known as Modern Australian cuisine.[[23]](#cite_note-23)\n\nArguably the first Modern Australian restaurant was Sydney's [Bayswater Brasserie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bayswater_Brasserie?action=edit&redlink=1 \"Bayswater Brasserie (page does not exist)\") (est. 1982), which offered Mediterranean dishes with Asian and Middle Eastern influences and \"showed Sydney [...] that food can be adventurous without being expensive\".[[24]](#cite_note-smh-24) The term itself was first used in print in the 1993 edition of the [Sydney Morning Herald](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sydney_Morning_Herald \"Sydney Morning Herald\") *Good Food Guide*,[[24]](#cite_note-smh-24)[[25]](#cite_note-25) which placed 34 restaurants under this heading, and was quickly adopted to describe the burgeoning food scene in Sydney in the 1990s.[[26]](#cite_note-26) Leading exponents of the style include [Tetsuya Wakuda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tetsuya_Wakuda \"Tetsuya Wakuda\"), [Neil Perry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neil_Perry \"Neil Perry\") and [Peter Gilmore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peter_Gilmore_(chef) \"Peter Gilmore (chef)\").[[27]](#cite_note-27) As of 2014, the term is considered somewhat dated, with many restaurants preferring to call their style \"contemporary Australian cuisine\" instead.[[28]](#cite_note-28)\n\n## Fruit and vegetables\n\n### Lamb will often form part of either a [Sunday roast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sunday_roast \"Sunday roast\") or a barbecue. It is also commonly found as an ingredient in [gyros](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gyro_(food) \"Gyro (food)\") (\"yiros\") and [doner kebabs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doner_kebab \"Doner kebab\"), brought by Greek and Turkish immigrants in the 1960s and 1970s. Australia consumes more lamb and mutton than any other country listed by the [OECD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/OECD \"OECD\")-[FAO](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FAO \"FAO\") (with [Kazakhstan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kazakhstan \"Kazakhstan\") in second place). In 2017, Australians consumed an average of 8.5 kilograms (19 lb) per person. By way of comparison, New Zealanders average 3.2 kilograms (7.1 lb) and Americans just 0.4 kilograms (0.88 lb).[[37]](#cite_note-37)\n\nLunch at an [Australian pub](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Australian_pub \"Australian pub\") is called a *[counter lunch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Counter_lunch?action=edit&redlink=1 \"Counter lunch (page does not exist)\")*, while the term *counter meal* is used for either lunch or dinner.[[38]](#cite_note-38) Common dishes served at counter lunches and counter meals are steak and chips, [chicken parmigiana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chicken_parmigiana \"Chicken parmigiana\") and chips, a mixed grill (an assortment of grilled meats), and roast lamb or beef with roast vegetables.[[39]](#cite_note-39) [Lunch meats](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lunch_meat \"Lunch meat\") are known as **smallgoods** in Australia and New Zealand, referring to products such as [ham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ham \"Ham\"), [bacon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bacon \"Bacon\"), [sausages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sausages \"Sausages\") or [salami](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salami \"Salami\").[[40]](#cite_note-40)\n\n### Game\n\n[Kangaroo meat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kangaroo_meat \"Kangaroo meat\") is available as [game](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Game_(hunting) \"Game (hunting)\") in Australia, although it is not among the most commonly eaten meats. In colonial-era recipes, kangaroo was treated much like [ox tail](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ox_tail \"Ox tail\"), and braised until tender, forming a rich gravy. It is available today in various cuts and sausages.[[41]](#cite_note-41)[[42]](#cite_note-42) Kangaroo is, however, a common commercial [dog food](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dog_food \"Dog food\") in Australia. Other less commonly eaten forms of game are [emu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emu \"Emu\") and [crocodile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crocodile_meat \"Crocodile meat\").\n\n![Kangaroo steak](//thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/a/ac/Kangaroo_steak_at_a_restaurant_in_Sydney%2C_Australia.jpg/250px-Kangaroo_steak_at_a_restaurant_in_Sydney%2C_Australia.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)\n![A crocodile dish](//thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/8/82/Krokodilmenu_fg1.jpg/250px-Krokodilmenu_fg1.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)\n\n## Fish and seafood\n\nSeafood consumption is increasing, but it is less common in the Australian diet than poultry and beef.[[19]](#cite_note-bulletin-19)\nAustralian cuisine features Australian seafood such as [southern bluefin tuna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_bluefin_tuna \"Southern bluefin tuna\"), [King George whiting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/King_George_whiting \"King George whiting\"), [Moreton Bay bugs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moreton_Bay_bugs \"Moreton Bay bugs\"), mud crab, [jewfish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glaucosoma_hebraicum \"Glaucosoma hebraicum\"), dhufish ([Western Australia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_Australia \"Western Australia\")) and [yabby](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Freshwater_yabby \"Freshwater yabby\"). [Barramundi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barramundi \"Barramundi\") is a fish found in northern Australian river systems. [Bay lobsters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thenus \"Thenus\"), better known in Australia as [Moreton Bay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moreton_Bay \"Moreton Bay\") bugs, are common in seafood restaurants, or may be served with steak as \"surf and turf\".[*[citation needed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed \"Wikipedia:Citation needed\")*]\n\nThe most common species of the aquaculture industry are salmon, tuna, oysters, and prawns. Fishing and aquaculture constitute Australia's fifth most valuable agricultural industry after wool, beef, wheat, and dairy.[[46]](#cite_note-46) Approximately 600 varieties of marine and freshwater seafood species are caught and sold in Australia for both local and overseas consumption. Meat pies, sausage rolls, and pasties are often found at milk bars, bakeries, and petrol stations, often kept hot in a [pie warmer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pie_warmer?action=edit&redlink=1 \"Pie warmer (page does not exist)\") or needing to be [microwaved](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Microwave_oven \"Microwave oven\"); meat pies are also a staple at [Australian Rules football](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Australian_Football_League \"Australian Football League\") matches.[[64]](#cite_note-64) Chiko Rolls, dim sims, and other foods that need to be [deep-fried](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deep_frying \"Deep frying\") are to be found at fish and chip shops, as these have the necessary [deep fryers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deep_fryer \"Deep fryer\") in which to cook them. [Bread rolls](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bread_roll \"Bread roll\"), with a variety of fillings, are a common alternative to sandwiches.\n\nAustralian hamburgers and [steak sandwiches](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steak_sandwich \"Steak sandwich\") are mainly to be found at [fish and chip](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fish_and_chip \"Fish and chip\") shops. Australian hamburgers consist of a grilled beef patty, served with shredded lettuce and sliced tomato in a (usually toasted) round bread roll or bun, along with [tomato sauce](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tomato_sauce#Tomato_ketchup \"Tomato sauce\") (less commonly, [barbecue sauce](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barbecue_sauce \"Barbecue sauce\")). Common options to add include bacon, cheese, fried onions, fried egg, a slice of [beetroot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beetroot \"Beetroot\"), and a slice of pineapple. A [slice of gherkin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pickled_cucumber \"Pickled cucumber\") is rarely included; these are a feature of burgers from American chains, not Australian.[[65]](#cite_note-65) Steak sandwiches come with the same options, but instead of a beef patty, they consist of a thin steak and are served in two slices of toast, not buns. Pizza has also become a popular takeaway food item in Australia.[[66]](#cite_note-66)\n\nCommonly found at community and fundraising events are [sausage sizzle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sausage_sizzle \"Sausage sizzle\") stalls – a stall with a barbecue hot plate on which sausages are cooked. At a sausage sizzle, the sausage is served in a slice of white bread, with or without tomato sauce, and with the option of adding fried onions, and eaten as a snack or as a light lunch. A sausage sizzle at a [polling station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polling_place \"Polling place\") on any Australian state or Federal election day has humorously become known as a [Democracy sausage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Democracy_sausage \"Democracy sausage\").[[67]](#cite_note-67) Similar stalls are held in the car parks of most [Bunnings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bunnings \"Bunnings\") hardware stores on weekends, by volunteers fund-raising for service clubs, charities, societies, or sporting groups.
