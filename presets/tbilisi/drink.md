What comes out is **shotis puri** — long, canoe-shaped bread with a crispy crust and soft, steaming center.\n\nGrab a loaf fresh from the oven, tear it apart while it's still hot, and eat it with a block of Imeretian cheese from the shop next door. There is a second branch at Mazniashvili Street 33.\n\nFor mountain khinkali, the bigger, coarser, pepperier kind made in the Khevsureti and Mtiuleti highlands, **Pictograma** on Atoneli Street cooks Khevsurian food and **Khash-Khash** in Chugureti does the Ghulelebi mountain style.\n\n## Modern Georgian: the new school\n\nTbilisi's modern restaurant scene barely existed a decade ago. Now it is one of the most interesting in the region. These kitchens take Georgian ingredients and technique and do something with them, sometimes brilliantly, sometimes too cleverly, but always worth your attention.\n\nBarbarestan\n\nEvery dish traces back to the 19th-century cookbook of the writer Barbare Jorjadze, cooked with modern technique. Chakapuli, asparagus soup, pkhali. Gault and Millau Georgia scores it 16.5 of 20.\n\nAghmashenebeli Ave 132, daily 14:00 to 23:00, reserve\n\nShavi Lomi\n\nThe Black Lion, where modern Georgian started: an old house with mismatched furniture, resident cats in the courtyard and a seasonal menu. Kubdari and the vegetable plates are the ones to order. Listed at 26 to 65 GEL a head.\n\nZurab Kvlividze St, Marjanishvili side, daily 13:00 to 02:00, reserve at weekends\n\nCafe Littera\n\nIn the garden of the Writers' House, a mansion built for the brandy maker Davit Sarajishvili. Chef Tekuna Gachechiladze's Georgian-fusion menu changes often. The most beautiful place to eat in the city.\n\nMachabeli St 13, daily 13:00 to 23:45, reserve days ahead\n\nOtsY\n\nCalls what it cooks \"Tbilisi cuisine\": the city's own historical dishes, with European technique. Small room on Shavteli Street in the Old Town, near Anchiskhati church.\n\nShavteli St 20, Old Town, reserve\n\nKeto and Kote\n\nUpscale Georgian classics in a house at the end of a Vera dead end. Good for a first-night dinner that still tastes Georgian.\n\nZandukeli dead end, Vera\n\nShushabandi and Unfound Door\n\nShushabandi in the Wine Factory N1 complex does Georgian with fine-dining technique. Unfound Door on Aghmashenebeli is modern Georgian with a European accent and opens at 08:00, so it doubles as breakfast.\n\nPetriashvili St 1, Vera; Aghmashenebeli Ave 111\n\n**Barbarestan** deserves a paragraph because it does something no other restaurant in Tbilisi does: it cooks from one historical source. Barbare Jorjadze's cookbook is the closest thing Georgia has to a founding kitchen text, and the restaurant brings her recipes back with modern technique and faithful flavours. Candles, antique mirrors, ceramics on the tables. It is at Aghmashenebeli Avenue 132, which is also one of the [best streets to walk in Tbilisi](/guides/tbilisi-first-time/).\n\n**Shavi Lomi** is where the city's food renaissance started. Before it opened, \"modern Georgian\" was not a category: you ate traditional or you ate foreign. The seasonal menu turns over and the natural wine list is one of the best in the city. It is on a quiet street on the Marjanishvili side of the river that you would not find without a map, which is part of the point. An earlier version of this page had it on Zubalashvili Street; it is on Zurab Kvlividze Street.\n\n## Regional kitchens\n\nGeorgia is a small country with enormous culinary range, and each region has dishes you will not find anywhere else. These restaurants cook one region's food, and for several of them it is the only way to try those dishes without travelling there.\n\n| Restaurant | Region | Order |\n| --- | --- | --- |\n| Amra | Abkhazia and Samegrelo | Ajika, elarji, gebzhalia |\n| Oro | Samegrelo | Kharcho, Megrelian khachapuri |\n| Chef Saradjeff | Meskheti | Atria, apokhti khinkali |\n| Kade | Mtiuleti | Mountain khinkali, stews |\n| Kakhelebi | Kakheti | Mtsvadi, seasonal salads |\n| Ghebi | Racha | Lobio, shkmeruli, 24 hours |\n| Retro | Adjara | Adjaruli khachapuri |\n| Leila's Khinkali | Pankisi (Kist) | Halal beef khinkali |\n\n**Amra** on Danelia Street in Saburtalo is the place to understand how different Georgian food is from region to region. Abkhazian and Megrelian cooking is the spiciest in the country, with more chilli, more garlic and adjika on everything. Order the elarji (cornmeal beaten with sulguni cheese until it stretches like fondue) and the gebzhalia (rolled cheese in a mint and yoghurt sauce). **Oro** at Marjanishvili Street 5 does the Megrelian repertoire in a more central room. **Chef Saradjeff**, below the baths at Gorgasali Street 11, is the only Meskhetian kitchen we know in the city. **Kade** (Tsinamdzghvrishvili Street 94, Chugureti) cooks the food of the Mtiuleti highlands, **Kakhelebi** (Beliashvili Street 41, and a bigger branch on the Kakheti Highway) does Kakhetian mtsvadi and salads, **Leila's Khinkali** (Shevchenko Street 10, Mtatsminda) is a Kist kitchen from the Pankisi valley with halal beef khinkali, and **Retro** (Gamrekeli Street 1, Saburtalo) is where Tbilisi goes for the boat-shaped Adjarian khachapuri.\n\n### Khashi: the morning soup\n\nKhashi is tripe and trotter broth, eaten early with garlic, vinegar and a shot of chacha, traditionally as a hangover cure. **Khasheria** at Samghebro Street 9 in Abanotubani, a modern Georgian kitchen named for it, reopened in June 2026 after a break. **Maspindzelo** at Gorgasali Street 7, on the edge of the bath district since 2008, is the old-school option: open 10:30 to midnight Sunday to Thursday and around the clock on Friday and Saturday, with live singing, big portions and a listed 20 to 70 GEL a head. It is the natural stop after the [sulfur baths](/culture/tbilisi-sulfur-baths-guide/).\n\n## Budget eats: a meal for under 20 GEL a head\n\nYou can eat extraordinarily well in Tbilisi for very little. These are the canteens, bakeries and no-frills rooms where drivers and builders eat. The food is honest, the portions are big, and nobody will ask whether you want sparkling or still.\n\n| Place and where | Notes |\n| --- | --- |\n| MapshaliaAghmashenebeli Ave 137 | Megrelian canteen: kharcho, elarji, lobio |\n| GhebiAghmashenebeli Ave 115 | Rachan, 24 hours |\n| Coca-Cola Dining RoomTsereteli Ave 114, Didube | Soviet-style mtsvadi and kharcho, cash only |\n| Dumplings N1 (Sapelmene N1)Ketevan Dedofali Ave 69a, Isani | Soviet pelmeni, 24 hours, cash only |\n| ChashnagiriKote Afkhazi St 25 and Vazha-Pshavela Ave 30 | Shkmeruli, khinkali, mtsvadi |\n| Tone bakeriesEvery neighbourhood | Shotis puri, lobiani, khachapuri, 3-8 GEL |\n| Dezerter Bazaar stallsTsinamdzghvrishvili St | Cheese, churchkhela, fruit for grazing |\n\n**Mapshalia** on Aghmashenebeli Avenue is the budget king: a Megrelian canteen where kharcho, elarji, lobio and mchadi (cornbread) come out fast and cheap, with decor that amounts to tables. If you measure a city by its cheapest good meal, Mapshalia is Tbilisi's answer. Down the same avenue at number 115, **Ghebi** cooks the Racha repertoire, lobio and shkmeruli first among it, around the clock.\n\nThe **tone bakeries** (a tone is the clay oven) in every neighbourhood are the city's real street food. Watch the baker slap dough onto the inside wall of the oven, then tear into the result while it burns your fingers. A lobiani (bean-stuffed bread) or a khachapuri from a neighbourhood tone costs 3 to 8 GEL in 2026 and is one of the best things you will eat in Georgia. The [markets guide](/food-wine/tbilisi-markets-guide/) covers the Dezerter Bazaar stalls.\n\n## Wine bars and wine restaurants\n\nGeorgia has been making wine for about 8,000 years, and Tbilisi's natural wine bar scene has grown fast in the last decade. These places take wine seriously without being solemn about it. For the background, read our [wine culture guide](/food-wine/georgian-wine-culture/); for bars that are mostly about drinking, the [wine bars page](/food-wine/best-wine-bars-tbilisi/).\n\n ![The lit bottle wall of a Tbilisi wine bar in the evening, with one person at the counter](/images/food-wine-best-restaurants-tbilisi-wine-bar.webp) \n\nTbilisi pours a lot of qvevri wine by the glass. At 8000 Vintages it starts at 6 GEL; a bottled wine by the glass is more often 10 to 15.\n\n \n\nVino Underground\n\nThe original Tbilisi natural wine bar, a cellar in Sololaki where a rotating cast of small Georgian producers is poured by the glass. Food is cheese and bread; you are here for the wine, and the staff know every winemaker.\n\nGalaktion Tabidze St 15; Sun to Thu 15:00 to 23:00, Fri and Sat 16:00 to midnight\n\n8000 Vintages\n\nWine shop and bar off Rustaveli with the widest by-the-glass list in the centre and guided tastings. Glasses from 6 GEL; sommelier tastings 55 GEL a person for two to five guests, daily 13:00 to 18:00 by reservation.\n\nTabukashvili St 27 and other branches\n\nPoliphonia\n\nNatural wine with a plant-based Georgian kitchen on Chonkadze Street in Mtatsminda. The one wine restaurant where a vegan eats as well as everyone else.\n\nChonkadze St 29\n\nSulico and Craft Wine Restaurant\n\nTwo wine restaurants rather than bars. Sulico in Vera showcases Georgian producers with a seasonal menu; Craft Wine Restaurant in Chugureti pairs Georgian dishes with a natural wine list.\n\nZandukeli St 27, Vera; Ninoshvili St 54, Chugureti\n\nDadi and Baasi\n\nDadi is a bar and shop just off Freedom Square. Baasi, new in 2026, is a warehouse-sized bar and shop at the far end of Sololaki with a small stage and live music on Friday and Saturday nights.\n\nDadiani St 4; Sololaki\n\nWine Merchants and the Atoneli wine window\n\nWine Merchants in Chugureti pours from 8 GEL a glass. The wine window on Atoneli Street, opened in spring 2025, hands a glass through a hatch for 8 GEL and is the cheapest taste of the natural scene.\n\nJavakhishvili St 46; Atoneli St\n\n## Special occasion restaurants\n\nSometimes you want tablecloths. Maybe it is an anniversary, maybe you just came off the [Tusheti road](/road-trips/tusheti-road-guide/) and feel you have earned it. These tables deliver a memorable evening at prices that would be moderate in any western European city: budget 150 to 200 GEL for two with wine, more at Reserve.\n\n| Restaurant | Why | Booking |\n| --- | --- | --- |\n| Cafe Littera | Writers' House garden, changing fusion menu | Days ahead in spring and autumn |\n| Barbarestan | 19th-century cookbook recipes, candlelit room | A few days ahead |\n| Reserve | New Nordic meets Georgian; tasting menu from 14:00 | Ahead |\n| Grand Cafe | Georgian-international in the Telegraph Hotel | Essential for dinner |\n| Bina N37 | Sea bream and Megrelian dishes in an apartment restaurant | Two or more days ahead |\n| AMO | Modern Georgian with the city below | Recommended |\n\n**Cafe Littera** is the standard. The Writers' House on Machabeli Street is one of the finest interiors in Tbilisi, and dinner in its garden in summer feels like a 19th-century literary salon that happens to have a kitchen. Gault and Millau Georgia rates it 15.5 of 20. Book a few days ahead for the garden in the spring and autumn peaks.\n\n**Reserve** at 9 April Street 3, behind the parliament, is the city's most ambitious kitchen, a New Nordic take on Georgian ingredients served as a tasting menu from 14:00. **AMO** at Kakabadze Street 27 in Mtatsminda does modern Georgian with the city spread out below. **Grand Cafe** in the Telegraph Hotel at Rustaveli Avenue 31 is the newest addition to this category and avoids the usual hotel-restaurant trap of being soulless and overpriced. The menu bridges Georgian and international (a dorado ceviche sits next to the Georgian plates) and dinner reservations are essential. **Bina N37** at Mgaloblishvili Street 5a is the odd one out: an apartment restaurant in Saburtalo known for whole sea bream and Megrelian dishes, with a menu that follows the day's shopping. It books out two or more days ahead.\n\n## Eating by neighbourhood\n\nWhere you sleep decides where you eat most nights. A quick breakdown to plan around your [accommodation](/destinations/where-to-stay-tbilisi/):\n\n| Neighbourhood | Our picks |\n| --- | --- |\n| Sololaki and the Old TownDense and touristy, with good rooms among the traps | Amo Rame, Klike's, OtsY, Vino Underground, Baasi, Iasamani |\n| Rustaveli and MtatsmindaCentral, mixed, easy from the big hotels | Salobie Bia, Sofia Melnikova's, Cafe Daphna, Pictograma, 8000 Vintages, Reserve |\n| Aghmashenebeli and MarjanishviliThe most varied strip in the city, old and new side by side | Barbarestan, Amo Rame Bani, Mapshalia, Ghebi, Oro, Shavi Lomi, Unfound Door |\n| VeraResidential, local, few tourists | Shemomechama, Beletage, Keto and Kote, Sulico, Shushabandi |\n| Saburtalo and DidubeWhere Tbilisi eats on a weeknight; worth the metro ride | Amra, Retro, Bina N37, Coca-Cola Dining Room, Sasadilo at Zeche |\n| AbanotubaniTourist-heavy, mostly overpriced, a few exceptions | Chef Saradjeff, Khasheria, Maspindzelo |\n\n## Tourist traps to avoid\n\nWe are not going to name restaurants here; they change too fast and some recover. These are the signs that reliably predict a bad meal in Tbilisi:\n\nA photo menu at the door\n\nLaminated picture menus on the pavement are the universal sign. Real restaurants do not need to lure you in with photos of khachapuri.\n\nA \"traditional show\" included\n\nIf dinner comes with folk dancing, the food is paying for the dancers. Go to a proper performance instead; our [music and dance guide](/culture/georgian-music-dance/) lists them.\n\nStaff outside recruiting\n\nSomeone at the door inviting you in? Keep walking. Good restaurants here have waiting lists, not greeters. This is concentrated on Shardeni Street and around the baths.\n\nNo Georgians inside\n\nThe simplest test. Look through the window before you sit. If every table is tourists, the kitchen is calibrated for people who do not know what the food should taste like.\n\nShardeni Street, mostly\n\nThe pedestrian street in the Old Town is pretty and is the epicentre of the city's tourist restaurants. Exceptions exist; scepticism is the default.\n\nAn English-only menu\n\nA menu with no Georgian on it means the restaurant does not expect Georgian customers. Good tourist-friendly places have bilingual menus.\n\nIf you are looking up a named restaurant's reviews before you book, especially in the Old Town, do it this way. Open the place on Google Maps rather than TripAdvisor, sort the reviews by newest, and read the Georgian-language ones with the translate button: they are written by people who know what the dishes should taste like. Then apply the six signs above to the street-view photo. A high rating built on a few dozen English reviews that all mention the live music is a sign of a show, not a kitchen.\n\n## How to order like you know what you are doing\n\nGeorgian restaurant culture has its own logic, and following it improves the meal.\n\n| Rule | Why |\n| --- | --- |\n| Order for the table | Meals are communal. Four to six dishes to share, not a main each. |\n| Cold first, then hot | Pkhali, badrijani and salads first, then khachapuri, khinkali and meat. That is how Georgians pace it. |\n| Do not over-order | Portions are big. Three or four dishes feed two people. You can always add. |\n| Ask what is fresh today | Many kitchens have off-menu dishes. Ra gakvt dghes, \"what do you have today\", gets you told. |\n| Take the house wine | At traditional places it comes in a jug from a small producer, from about 5 GEL a glass, and is often better than the bottled list. |\n| Finish with tea or coffee | Restaurant desserts are the weak point. The sweets you want are at bakeries and the market. |\n\nOrdering is easier when you can read the menu and name the dishes. EasyGeorgian's [guide to 25 Georgian dishes beyond khachapuri and khinkali](https://easygeorgian.com/blog/georgian-food-guide/) gives each one its Georgian name with the pronunciation, which is the difference between pointing at the menu and asking for the ostri.\n\n## Vegetarian and vegan eating\n\nGeorgia is quietly one of the best countries in Europe for vegetarians. The Orthodox fasting calendar means a large share of the traditional repertoire is plant-based by design, not as an afterthought, and Georgians eat it every week. The fully plant-based cafes, the fasting-menu vocabulary and the traps (egg in dough, butter on khinkali, ham in lobio) are in our [vegetarian and vegan guide](/food-wine/vegetarian-vegan-georgia/).\n\nAlways available\n\nPkhali (walnut and vegetable pastes), lobio (bean stew), badrijani nigvzit (walnut-stuffed aubergine), ajapsandali (a ratatouille), mushroom or potato khinkali, Imeretian khachapuri (vegetarian, not vegan), jonjoli (pickled bladdernut buds).\n\nDedicated kitchens\n\nIasamani at Asatiani Street 33 in Sololaki cooks vegetarian and vegan Georgian, khachapuri chips included. Poliphonia in Mtatsminda pairs a plant-based menu with natural wine. Most modern Georgian restaurants have real vegetable sections, and during Lent even traditional places expand them.\n\n## Common mistakes\n\nFilling up on bread\n\nGeorgian bread is very good and arrives first, but it is dense. If you finish the shotis puri before the food arrives you will miss the best of the meal.\n\nKhachapuri and khinkali at the same meal\n\nBoth are heavy and filling. Order both plus other dishes and half the table goes back. Pick one per meal and fill the rest with lighter plates.\n\nEating only in Abanotubani\n\nThe bath district has the worst restaurant-to-trap ratio in the city. Cross the river to Aghmashenebeli, or walk up to Vera, for better food at lower prices.\n\nSkipping the lemonade\n\nGeorgian limonati is a family of fizzy drinks, tarragon, pear, cream soda and grape among them. Order one instead of a cola. Tarragon (tarkhuna) is the most distinctive.\n\nBooking the folk-show restaurant for the food\n\nEthno Tsiskvili and the other big riverside show restaurants are a night out with a stage, priced for tour groups. Go for the dancing if you want it; do not go for the cooking.\n\nTrusting the wrong reviews\n\nIn Tbilisi, Google Maps ratings track reality better than TripAdvisor. Read the recent Georgian-language reviews with the translate button for the honest takes.\n\n## Practical information\n\n| Topic | Details |\n| --- | --- |\n| Service charge and tipping | Many Tbilisi bills print a 10 percent service charge (sometimes 12 or 15) below the 18 percent VAT. A canteen meal for two is about 40 GEL. The top tables such as Cafe Littera, Barbarestan and Reserve run 150 to 200 GEL for two with wine. Eating alone, budget 30 to 45 GEL because portions are shared-size. Khinkali cost 1.50 to 3 GEL a piece.\n\n  Do I need reservations at Tbilisi restaurants?  \n\nFor traditional restaurants, no, just walk in. For Shavi Lomi, Barbarestan, Cafe Littera, Reserve, Grand Cafe and Bina N37, book at least a day or two ahead, and more for Friday and Saturday dinners. Most places take reservations by phone or an Instagram message.\n\n  Is tipping expected at restaurants in Tbilisi?  \n\nCheck the bill first. Many Tbilisi restaurants print a 10 percent service charge, and when it is there no extra tip is expected. When it is not, leaving about 10 percent in cash is polite but not obligatory. Card terminals have no tip line.\n\n  What time do Georgians eat dinner?  \n\nLate. Dinner starts around 20:00 or 21:00 and restaurants stay busy until 23:00 or midnight. Lunch is roughly 13:00 to 15:00. A dining room often does not find its atmosphere until after 21:00.\n\n  What is the best restaurant in Tbilisi?  \n\nFor a first Georgian dinner, Shemomechama at Mtskheta Street 8 in Vera: the whole traditional menu cooked properly, 30 to 40 GEL a head, walk in. For the dinner you dress up for, Barbarestan at Aghmashenebeli Avenue 132, cooked from a 19th-century cookbook, about 75 to 100 GEL a head, booked a few days ahead. The full top ten is ranked at the top of this page.\n\n  Where do locals eat in Tbilisi?  \n\nAway from the Old Town. On a weeknight Tbilisi eats on Aghmashenebeli Avenue (Mapshalia, Ghebi, Barbarestan, Amo Rame Bani), in Vera (Shemomechama, Beletage) and in Saburtalo (Amra, Retro, Bina N37). The simplest test anywhere is to look through the window: if every table is Georgian, the kitchen is honest.\n\n  Where should I eat if I only have one night in Tbilisi?  \n\nShemomechama for classic Georgian done well, or Barbarestan for the elevated version cooked from a 19th-century cookbook. Either one gives you the range of Georgian flavours in a single sitting. If you want khinkali, Amo Rame in Sololaki.\n\n  Which restaurants are open late or all night?  \n\nCafe Daphna stays open through the night at weekends, Ghebi on Aghmashenebeli and Dumplings N1 in Isani run 24 hours, and Maspindzelo by the baths is open around the clock on Friday and Saturday. Shavi Lomi serves until 02:00.\n\n## Keep planning\n\n[![A Georgian table with Adjarian khachapuri, a board of khinkali, pkhali with pomegranate seeds, tomato and cucumber salad and shoti
