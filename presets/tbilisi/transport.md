A digital licence alone may not be recognised in Georgia, and rental companies may refuse to hand over the car without the physical document.**\n\n**Thinking about hiring a car?** My [full guide to renting a car in Georgia](https://wander-lush.org/driving-in-georgia-car-rental-tbilisi/) covers when it makes sense, what to watch out for, insurance requirements, and my tips for navigating the driving style.\n\n![A woman stands with a rental 4WD on a mountain road in Georgia.](data:image/svg+xml,%3Csvg%20xmlns='http://www.w3.org/2000/svg'%20viewBox='0%200%20900%20600'%3E%3C/svg%3E)Driving in Racha.\n\n## Consider coach buses for airport transfers & select intercity journeys\n\nCoach buses fill a small but useful gap in Georgia’s intercity transport system. They are not available nationwide, and they do not replace marshrutkas (yet) – but on the routes where they do operate, coaches are a comfortable, arguably safer alternative to vans.\n\nCoach services are currently available for longer intercity trips on major corridors (such as Tbilisi-Batumi, [Tbilisi-Kutaisi](https://wander-lush.org/kutaisi-to-tbilisi-bus-train/) and [Tbilisi-Zugdidi](https://wander-lush.org/tbilisi-to-zugdidi-train/)), and for [Kutaisi International Airport transfers](https://wander-lush.org/kutaisi-airport-to-city/) to cities and resort areas. For most routes, there are 2-5 services a day.\n\nThe main operators are [Georgian Bus](https://georgianbus.com/), [Omnibus](https://omnibusexpress.ge/), [Metro Georgia](https://metrogeorgia.ge/) and [CityBus](https://citybus.ge/en). There are no major differences between the four. Vehicles are modern, with comfortable seating, AC, free WIFI and charging outlets, and generous luggage storage in the hold.\n\n![On board a modern coach bus in Georgia, with reclining seats and curtains, as it pulls out of Tbilisi city.](data:image/svg+xml,%3Csvg%20xmlns='http://www.w3.org/2000/svg'%20viewBox='0%200%20900%20600'%3E%3C/svg%3E)On board a Georgian Bus coach.\n\n## Look for shared transfers on popular routes\n\nShared transfers are a handy upgrade for some high-demand routes provided you are travelling during the high season. They are cheaper than hiring a car outright, but generally more dependable and comfortable than a marshrutka.\n\nDesigned for tourists rather than locals, departures are pre-arranged, and pickup points are clearer than with informal road transport. Shared transfers work particularly well for mountain destinations and resort areas – for example [Kutaisi-Mestia](https://wander-lush.org/tbilisi-to-svaneti-how-to-get-to-mestia/) or [Tbilisi-Gudauri](https://wander-lush.org/tbilisi-to-gudauri/).\n\nOne of the more established operators is [Budget Georgia](https://www.budget-georgia.com/), with offices in Kutaisi and Tbilisi. Email them and mention me for a 10% discount on any of their tours or transfers.\n\n## Don’t count on tourist shuttles\n\nUnlike some countries, Georgia does not have a network of tourist shuttle buses. At present, there’s only one: the [Gareji Line shuttle](https://www.facebook.com/gareji.line/) from Tbilisi to [David Gareja Monastery](https://wander-lush.org/david-gareja-monastery-georgia/). It only operates during the tourist season.\n\nBeyond this, destinations that travellers sometimes assume will have shuttle access – such as [Vardzia](https://wander-lush.org/vardzia-cave-monastery-guide/) or the [canyons and caves around Kutaisi](https://wander-lush.org/kutaisi-caves-canyons-day-trip/) – actually require a guided tour or private transport. Dedicated tourist shuttles simply aren’t part of the system.\n\n## Use domestic flights sparingly\n\nDomestic flights do exist, but they are best treated as a bonus.\n\nAir services connect a small handful of domestic airports – in Natakhtari (outside Tbilisi), Mestia, [Ambrolauri (Racha)](https://wander-lush.org/visit-racha-oni-travel-guide/) and Kutaisi – and are operated exclusively by one carrier, [Vanilla Sky](https://ticket.vanillasky.ge/en). The aircraft used are small Let L-410 turboprops, and while the flights are spectacularly scenic, they’re also highly weather-dependent.\n\nFlights operate under visual flight rules, which means departures rely on visibility rather than instruments. In practice, this makes the schedule highly vulnerable to Georgia’s changeable mountain weather. Same-day delays and cancellations are common, particularly for routes to Mestia and Racha, and tickets can be difficult to secure even when services are running as scheduled.\n\nFor travellers on a tight itinerary, domestic flights are not dependable. Road transport is more predictable – even if it takes much longer.\n\n![A small plane landing at Mestia Airport in Georgia, seen through a wire fence.](data:image/svg+xml,%3Csvg%20xmlns='http://www.w3.org/2000/svg'%20viewBox='0%200%20600%20900'%3E%3C/svg%3E)![The unusual terminal building of the Mestia Airport in Svaneti, Georgia.](data:image/svg+xml,%3Csvg%20xmlns='http://www.w3.org/2000/svg'%20viewBox='0%200%20600%20900'%3E%3C/svg%3E)\n\n## Approach ride sharing & hitchhiking with care\n\nRide sharing and hitchhiking in Georgia are definitely possible, but like anywhere, should be approached with caution.\n\nBlaBlaCar does not operate in Georgia, and there is no comparable local service. Instead, most ride-sharing arrangements are made through social media channels – the most popular being the [Let’s Go Facebook Group](https://www.facebook.com/groups/1576613409281979). While this group can be useful, it’s important to understand that many posts are made by professional drivers, not private individuals – so payment might be expected.\n\nHitchhiking is culturally accepted in most areas, but you should be vigilant, especially in the mountains and on remote roads. Hitchhiking comes with a higher risk of encountering unsafe vehicles or drivers who’ve been drinking.\n\n* * *\n\n## Quick tips for transport in Georgia\n\n### Timetables, tickets & online reservations\n\n[TKT.GE](https://tkt.ge/en) is a reliable and intuitive booking platform that you can use to reserve train tickets. You can also use it to pre-purchase marshrutka tickets on select routes departing from Ortachala Station in Tbilisi.\n\nTrain tickets are also sold on [the official Georgian Railway website](https://gr.com.ge/). Tickets do sell out – especially in summer and around [holiday periods](https://wander-lush.org/festivals-in-georgia-country-calendar/) – so I recommend booking as far in advance as possible (up to 20 days for most routes).\n\nYou _cannot_ pre-book tickets for most marshrutka services – sometimes the fare is paid at a ticket office on the spot, otherwise it goes directly to the driver (always in cash). On routes that don’t accept pre-sales, you should plan to arrive at the station 30-45 minutes ahead of your intended departure time to ensure you get a seat.\n\nI have collected timetable and fare information for [marshrutka services departing from Didube (Okirba) Bus Station](https://wander-lush.org/didube-bus-station-tbilisi/), the main transport hub in Tbilisi. It also contains a video tour to help you find your van.\n\nCoach buses can be booked direct on the company website (as linked above), at the airport kiosk for Kutaisi transfers, or via [12Go](https://12go.asia/?z=1465377).\n\n### Seasonal routes\n\nSome mountain roads in Georgia are only open for a short window in summer, typically from late June to early October, depending on snowmelt and other conditions. This applies to [Tusheti](https://wander-lush.org/visit-tusheti-georgia-tips-guide/) and [Khevsureti](https://wander-lush.org/khevsureti-itinerary-travel-tips/), as well as some valleys around [Kazbegi](https://wander-lush.org/visit-kazbegi-georgia-travel-guide-tips/) and the high-altitude part of Racha. Outside this period, roads may be completely impassable or officially closed.\n\nEven during the season, access can be disrupted at short notice by landslides, heavy rain, or road works. Schedules for marshrutkas and shared transfers in these regions are highly weather-dependent and may change from day to day.\n\nIf you’re travelling in shoulder season or winter, it’s wise to [build more flexibility into your itinerary](https://wander-lush.org/ultimate-georgia-itinerary-country-europe/) and avoid tight connections. Hiring a professional driver is often the safest and most dependable option for longer or more exposed routes. [GoTrip](https://gotrip.ge/en/?p=wander-lush) drivers automatically use winter tires, and experienced local drivers are better equipped to assess road conditions and adapt plans if needed.\n\nAs a general rule, if a destination sits high, remote, or at the end of a long mountain road, always check current access conditions locally before committing to your plans. The [official GeoRoad Facebook Page](https://www.facebook.com/georoad.ge) is a helpful resource.\n\n### Travelling with luggage\n\nLuggage space is limited on marshrutka vans, so you will need to arrive early to secure a spot for your bag. If you are travelling with a large suitcase or oversized items (bicycles, skis, a stroller) then private transfer, train or coach is a safer option.\n\nGeorgian Railway claims to have a luggage limit, but I have never seen this enforced.\n\n### Travelling with kids\n\nChildren are welcome on all forms of transport in Georgia, but for comfort and safety reasons, I would advise against using marshrutka vans if you are travelling with young kids.\n\n[GoTrip](https://gotrip.ge/en/?p=wander-lush) can provide child safety seats on request, making it a great option for travelling with little ones. [Local Rent](https://localrent.com/en/georgia/?r=4610) offers infant safety seats and booster seats with its rental cars. Kids under five travel free on trains.\n\n* * *\n\n#### You might also be interested in…\n\n- [The ultimate Georgia itinerary](https://wander-lush.org/ultimate-georgia-itinerary-country-europe/): Four detailed & custom-designed itineraries\n- [Georgia Travel Guide](https://wander-lush.org/georgia-travel-guide/): All of my 200+ posts plus my top travel tips\n- [Georgia travel tips](https://wander-lush.org/georgia-country-travel-tips-first-time-visitors/): 25 essential things to know before you go\n- [Places to visit in Georgia](https://wander-lush.org/best-unique-places-to-visit-in-georgia-country/): 50+ unique & underrated destinations around the country\n\n- [The best things to do in Tbilisi](https://wander-lush.org/unique-things-to-do-in-tbilisi-georgia/): Favourites, hidden gems & local picks\n- [35+ best restaurants in Tbilisi](https://wander-lush.org/tbilisi-georgia-food-best-restaurants-cafes-guide/): Where to eat Georgian food\n- [15 best day trips from Tbilisi](https://wander-lush.org/best-day-trips-from-tbilisi-georgia/): With detailed transport instructions\n- [The best time to visit Georgia](https://wander-lush.org/best-time-to-visit-georgia-country/): Month-by-month guide to weather, festivals & events\n\n### Leave a Reply [Cancel reply](https://wander-lush.org/getting-around-georgia-intercity-transport/\\#respond)\n\nYour email address will not be published.Required fields are marked \\*\n\nComment \\*\n\nName \\*\n\nEmail \\*\n\n_All comments are moderated and may take a little while to appear. You can buy a card that is valid for a day, a week, a month, even a year!\n\nYou pay a set fee, and then you can use it all day everyday with a maximum cap of 20 uses per day.\n\n### How to Buy Your Card\n\nInside all metro stations throughout Tbilisi, you will find Metro Money kiosks. Tell them which card you want, and hand over 2 GEL (it’s the same price for both cards).\n\nYou have to pay with cash to buy the initial card. You do not need to show any form of ID as the card itself is not registered or linked to you.\n\n### How to Top Up Your Card & How Much to Top Up\n\nAt the same kiosks within the metro stations, you can hand over cash to be added on to your card.\n\nThere are top-up machines on the streets all around the city that you can also use to add credit to your transport cards.\n\nThey have a free-standing ATM look about them. At these machines, you can use cash or card to top up, but be aware that you won’t get any change at these machines; the full value of the note you insert will be added to your card.\n\n- Each card tap on the metro and buses costs 1 GEL. Cable cars can be a little extra, up to 2.5 GEL. Use this as a guide to work out how much credit to add to your card.\n\n## Savvy Traveller Pro Tips 🇬🇪\n\nAfter exploring Georgia for nine months, these are our favourite booking tools that we You do not need to tap again to leave.\n\nA flat fee is charged on your single tap, so whether you get off after one stop or six stops, you pay the same amount.\n\nOn buses, the card readers are within the bus, so you don’t tap before you get on. It’s very much a case of getting on the bus as a priority and then tapping your card later.\n\nAs it’s a flat fee, you pay the same regardless of when you tap on your journey.\n\nIf you tap your card on multiple legs of a journey within 90-minutes, the subsequent taps will not be charged.\n\nSo, say you travel on the metro, and then jump on a bus, the fee will still be the flat 1 GEL if taken within the 90-minute window from the first tap.\n\n![20250305 111701 scaled](https://relentlessroaming.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/04/20250305_111701-scaled.webp)\n\n### How Many People Can Use One Card?\n\nSo, I’m not aware of an official cut-off number, but I have used our card for me, Chris and two friends who came to visit us all in one journey.\n\nI would imagine that the 90-minute window rule would only apply to the first tap, but if you have tried it out, let us know!\n\n### Do You Actually Need a Transport Card?\n\nIt’s actually not a necessity! The card readers on buses, metro, and cable cars will accept other cards, but you will be charged a little extra.\n\nIt depends on how much you will be using public transport during your visit. If you’re only going to be in the city for a long weekend and plan on walking everywhere, except a scenic cable car ride, then you might as well just tap your card from back home.\n\nA tap on a metro/bus with a Metro Money card costs 1 GEL, whereas a tap from a credit card costs 1.5 GEL.\n\nThis means that you would need to tap your card 4 times to make your money back on the initial payment for the physical card.\n\n## Metro\n\nTbilisi metro is efficient and clean and really pretty good. Trains run regularly (every 5 minutes or so) between 6 AM and midnight.\n\nBuilt during the Soviet Union, the metro stations are deep and many have the lavish decoration that ex-Soviet metro stations are renowned for.\n\nThe only other thing with ex-Soviet metro stations is that they tend to have ruling on photography; don’t ask me why. The red Akhmeteli-Varkeltili Line has 16 stops and runs north to south through Tbilisi, while the green Saburtalo Line has 7 stops and runs west from the Tbilisi Central train station to the university.\n\nThe only stop where the two metros connect is Station Square which is at Tbilisi Central train station, so if you need to switch lines you have to do it here.\n\n### Metro Tips\n\nThe metro stations tend not to have their station names written in a way that is visible to the passenger on the train. So, when you’re trying to check if this is the station where you need to jump off, it can be a little tricky.\n\nThere is, however, an announcement just before each stop, which is said in Georgian _and_ English, so make sure you listen out.\n\nAs a failsafe, count how many stops on the map on the station/in the train and count as you go!\n\n## Buses\n\nBuses are modern and look like any you would get in any other European city. Jump on the bus, tap your card, and then jump on when you get to your destination.\n\nIt’s pretty straight forward. If you want to double check you’re getting on the right bus, I usually say my destination to the driver as a question.\n\nYou will get a funny look because they notoriously do not like to speak to anyone, but we have always at least got a nod or shake of the head from the driver. Along a similar vein, drivers do not deal with any tickets and will not accept cash payment.\n\nGoogle Maps has good info on bus stops and schedules with live tracking on the buses. Traffic around Tbilisi can get a bit mad, so if you _can_ avoid the buses and get there by metro, definitely avoid the bus.\n\nIf you’re flying into/out of Tbilisi during the day time, there is a [**bus that runs between the city centre and the airport**](https://relentlessroaming.com/tbilisi-airport-to-the-city-centre/).\n\nIt’ll usually get you there in half an hour, and it only costs 1 GEL if you tap your Metro Money card!\n\n## Marshrutka\n\nNow, we have never taken a marshrutka in Tbilisi, only to travel to other parts of the country.
