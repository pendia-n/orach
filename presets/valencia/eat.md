Their paella Valenciana is a specialty, and comes according to the traditional recipe with *garrofó* (a large variety of butter bean), their green bean counterparts (like runner beans but larger), and plenty of aromatic rosemary and saffron.\n\n![Valencia restaurants - La Pepica paella](https://www.amsterdamfoodie.nl/wp-content/uploads/2018/11/Valencia-restaurants-La-Pepica-paella-500x400.jpg)\n\nValencia – home of paella!\n\n### Maremar\n\nIf you prefer your paella with seafood, you’ve got plenty of options along the beach too. We cycled in the opposite direction, down south to Playa de Pinedo, which felt like the quieter half of the Valencian coastline. Stopping at [Restaurante Maremar](https://www.tripadvisor.com/Restaurant_Review-g13424828-d14775312-Reviews-or300-Restaurante_Maremar-Pinedo_Valencia_Province_of_Valencia_Valencian_Commu.html) for a restorative cola, we decided it looked so nice that we’d pop back for lunch a couple of hours later. Their paella was everything you’d expect: ocean flavoured and sticky on the bottom of the pan. But their charcoal-grilled vegetables were possibly the stars of the show. Not to mention the uninterrupted views over the ocean…\n\n### Casa Roberto\n\nIf you don’t have quite the time or the energy to head to the beach and are staying more centrally, [Casa Roberto’s “house of rice”](http://casaroberto.es/) is a good choice in the Gran Vía neighbourhood. Inside, it’s quite traditional but we sat outside on the sunny pavement terrace to watch the world go by. Roberto’s paella Valenciana came stuffed with all the things you’d expect (chicken, rabbit, beans) but also artichoke and snails, which made it feel a little different. Their grilled vegetables were also excellent, if somewhat pricey, and they served a nice white wine (Rueda) by the glass.\n\n![](https://www.amsterdamfoodie.nl/wp-content/uploads/2021/11/Casa-Roberto-paella-in-Valencia-500x500.jpg)\n\nCasa Roberto’s paella Valenciana\n\n## Tapas bars in Valencia\n\nThe Valencians typically don’t go out for dinner until after 9 pm, and if you’re anything like me, you can’t possibly wait that long to eat. Luckily, the tapas bars are generally open earlier, so you can stop by for a snack and a drink before dinner, or turn it into a full meal.\n\n### ***Taberna Pare Pere***\n\nAn ultra-local tapas bar just inside the Russafa neighbourhood, you won’t see many tourists in [Taberna Pare Pere](https://www.cantonetparepere.com/). This simple spot is known for its fish and seafood: think just-seared tuna fillets with pistachio sauce, or langoustines cooked with *ajo tierno* (young garlic shoots that taste somewhere between a spring onion and wild garlic). The menu seems to be a general guideline rather than an accurate list: while several of the items weren’t available, many other (unwritten) daily specials were. So it’s worth asking what’s on offer before you get too far in your decision-making process.\n\n![](https://www.amsterdamfoodie.nl/wp-content/uploads/2023/11/Taber-Pare-Pere-Valencia-tapas-bar-500x830.jpg)\n\nTaberna Pare Pere’s speciality is tuna\n\n### Casa Montana\n\nLocated in El Cabanyal near the coast, you’ll find queues out the door to get a table at well-known [Casa Montana](https://www.emilianobodega.com/) so be sure to reserve in advance. This place is ultra-traditional, but in the best possible way: mouth-watering *jamón Iberico*, silky Rioja and steaming piles of mussels. They also specialise in artisan canned fish, so we tried the razor clams – I was sceptical but they were briny and good. Also delicious were the young broad beans fried with *lomo*, the tuna cured in seven spices, and the red *piquillo* peppers stuffed with cheese, coated in flour and deep-fried. Go hungry because you’ll want to order everything on the menu!\n\n![Casa Montana - tapas restaurant Valencia](https://www.amsterdamfoodie.nl/wp-content/uploads/2018/11/Casa-Montana-tapas-restaurant-Valencia-500x625.jpg)\n\nCasa Montana’s traditional tapas\n\n### La Pilareta\n\nIn Valencia’s central El Carmen neighbourhood, picture-perfect Roman and Gothic buildings nuzzle up against colourful street art and sunny terraces. But one of the more traditional bars in the area is [La Pilareta](http://www.barlapilareta.es/en/), where the mussel shells are still thrown into crates in front of the bar as the staff have done for decades. Better than the mussels, however, were the *montaditos*: halved, toasted bread rolls topped with grated tomato plus other variations. We tried the *morcilla* (blood sausage) and the chorizo, which tasted a lot better than they looked!\n\n### El Rodamón de Russafa\n\nFor a modern, creative, fusion version of tapas and wine, head over to the hipster, minimalist [El Rodamón de Russafa](https://elrodamon.com/). On the menu, you’ll be greeted with some Asian flavours in the form of sweet & sour aubergine or duck spring rolls alongside Spanish *pulpo* and calamari. On the wine list, you’ll find Spanish bottles next to other European wines at a variety of price points. It’s worth taking a look at the huge array of bottles displayed at the far end of the restaurant. We loved the Priorat: a Garnacha-led blend from Catalunya that was full-bodied and boozy but still fruity and balanced.\n\n![](https://www.amsterdamfoodie.nl/wp-content/uploads/2021/11/El-Rodamon-de-Russafa-500x682.jpg)\n\nSalt & pepper squid and Priorat: El Rodamón de Russafa\n\n## Modern Spanish restaurants in Valencia\n\nOf course, food in Valencia is not all about paella and tapas. There’s some seriously creative cooking going on as well, led by an innovative new wave of Spanish chefs. As evidenced by the growing number of recommendations in this section…\n\n### ***Timoteo Gastrobar***\n\nLocated in Arrancapins, just west of Valencia’s old town, [Timoteo Gastrobar](https://timoteogastro.com/) is serving up a Spanish-international fusion of family-style sharing plates. Patatas bravas came with *sobrasada* – a sort of spicy, cured sausage meat that’s a bit like ‘*nduja*. Roasted aubergine was smoky and savoury, with what tasted like a sesame-miso sauce, while tuna *tataki* had equally Asian accents with ginger and sesame. Finally, the jamón “bikini” in brioche bread echoed a Cuban sandwich. The service was a little chaotic, but it’s forgivable for food this good.\n\n![](https://www.amsterdamfoodie.nl/wp-content/uploads/2023/11/Timoteo-Gastrobar-Valencia-500x302.jpg)\n\nSpanish-international fusion fare at Timoteo Gastrobar\n\n### ***Agua Salada***\n\nLiterally translating as “salt water”, it’s no surprise that [Agua Salada](https://aguasaladavalencia.com/) specialises in fish and seafood. And while I’m sure these are locally caught, the flavours borrow from many different cultures. Cuttlefish came in wasabi mayo, while shrimp tartare was creamy and spicy with perhaps a touch of shiso. Tuna *tataki* (seemingly a favourite on Valencian menus) was served with giant capers and za’atar-style seasoning, while baby scallops came with a soy-based sauce. For those in search of something more traditional, the squid stew tasted resolutely Mediterranean, while the smoked fish appeared in a Russian salad format that we saw a lot elsewhere. Agua Salada was one of my favourite meals from my most recent trip, and is well worth the trek outside of the city centre.\n\n![](https://www.amsterdamfoodie.nl/wp-content/uploads/2023/11/Agua-Salada-restaurant-Valencia-500x761.jpg)\n\nAgua Salada specialises in fish and seafood\n\n### ***El Aprendiz de Tapas***\n\nOff the beaten path in the Benimaclet neighbourhood, [El Aprendiz de Tapas](https://elaprendiztapas.com/) is a delightful combination of cool minimalism and colourful flowers. It also has what looks to be a fantastic terrace in good weather, overlooking a quiet square. The menu is eclectic, but somehow the kitchen nails absolutely everything. European flavours included smoky aubergine with mozzarella and mint pesto, or slow-cooked beef with couscous. Asia was represented by Chinese-style pork dumplings and Thai-style pork ribs with the most fragrant yellow curry sauce. Even traces of *nikkei* were present in the form of tuna ceviche (served seared, “caliente” style, with plantain chips and cancha corn) and salmon *tataki* with sesame and kimchi. It sounds like a lot could go wrong, but trust me when I say that it was all spot on. Even the desserts were a joy: the most unctuously creamy, baked cheesecake, plus a refreshing yuzu mousse with mango sorbet. Perhaps because of its up-and-coming location, the bill at the end was staggeringly reasonable. Go before they put the prices up!\n\n![](https://www.amsterdamfoodie.nl/wp-content/uploads/2021/11/El-Aprendiz-de-Tapas-Valencia-500x628.jpg)\n\nThai pork ribs at El Aprendiz de Tapas\n\n### Canalla Bistro\n\nChef Ricard Camarena holds two Michelin stars at his eponymous restaurant. But for those of us who don’t have the funds to splash on Michelin-starred dining, Camarena also runs the more affordable [Canalla Bistro](https://www.canallabistro.com/) on the outskirts of Russafa. Think international fusion dishes that aren’t afraid of bold, spicy flavours. I particularly enjoyed the Peruvian-style seabass ceviche and the Asian-style tuna tartare, but literally everything we ordered was delicious – including the veggie dishes like spicy aubergine and vegetable tempura. Despite Camarena’s culinary cachet, dinner at his bistro was extremely affordable (especially by northern European standards).\n\n![Valencia restaurants - Ricard Camarena Cannalla Bistro](https://www.amsterdamfoodie.nl/wp-content/uploads/2018/11/Valencia-restaurants-Ricard-Camarena-Cannalla-Bistro-500x375.jpg)\n\nSeabass ceviche and tuna tartar at Canalla Bistro\n\n### La Tasqueta del Mercat\n\nIn the same neighbourhood and a good alternative if you can’t get into Canalla Bistro is [La Tasqueta del Mercat](https://www.tripadvisor.com/Restaurant_Review-g187529-d4887788-Reviews-La_Tasqueta_Del_Mercat-Valencia_Province_of_Valencia_Valencian_Community.html) – close to Russafa market, as the name suggests. Each lunch and dinner time, they have a few set menus that are extremely good value for money. We paid under €25 for a five-course menu (this little Amsterdammer’s jaw dropped!) plus only a few extra euros for a fabulous bottle of Ribera del Duero. I especially liked the *bacalao* croquettes that were crunchy on the outside and fluffy on the inside, as well as the tuna tartare with chilli and peanut sauces served with crisp corn tortilla. Plus, the chicken-liver pté with focaccia and pickled onions was simple but perfectly prepared.\n\n### Karak\n\nBang in the centre of the old city, modern fine-dining [restaurant Karak](http://restaurantekarak.com/) is the brainchild of the extremely talented Rakel Cernicharo. We tried her ten-course tasting menu, which was a creative fusion of European and Asian flavours – playful but still recognisable. One of my favourite dishes was the shrimp ravioli with red curry sauce, seaweed and a garnish of fascinatingly sour green leaves – a veritable flavour bomb. Beef *tataki* with *beurre noisette*, hazelnut sauce and leeks (pictured below) was delicate and subtle by contrast, but no less delicious for that. While Karak’s signature dish of salmon cured for four days in soy sauce with sushi rice and kimchi cream was an umami hit. What’s more, the sommelier was very knowledgeable about suitable wines to pair with the dishes, and you get a great view of the kitchen if you sit at the long communal tables upstairs.\n\n![Karak - fine dining in Valencia](https://www.amsterdamfoodie.nl/wp-content/uploads/2018/11/Karak-fine-dining-in-Valencia-500x625.jpg)\n\nFine dining at Karak\n\n## International food in Valencia\n\nYou probably didn’t come to Valencia looking for Indian curry, or Italian pizza, or Argentinian steak. But once you’ve gorged yourself on tapas and paella for a few days, you might be ready for something different…\n\n### Kathmandu\n\nSince the first time I visited Valencia, there seems to have been an explosion of Indian restaurants – and some of them are really surprisingly good. One such eatery is [Kathmandu](https://kathmanducanovas.com/en/), in the Gran Vía neighbourhood, which specialises in Nepalese dishes as well as traditional North Indian fare. I especially enjoyed the *chana* masala (chickpea curry) and the special Nepali curry with tamarind and peppers. But everything was great, right down to the naan bread and the service.\n\n### Asador Argentino Gordon 10\n\nEssentially an Argentinian steakhouse, [Asador Argentino Gordon 10](https://www.gordon10.com/) is filled with British and Dutch tourists – but don’t let that put you off! One for the meat eaters, the kitchen serves up a truly excellent ribeye steak, done simply with baked potatoes and grilled vegetables. The wine is affordable and the service some of the best we had. A carnivore’s paradise.\n\n![](https://www.amsterdamfoodie.nl/wp-content/uploads/2021/11/Asador-Argentino-Gordon-10-steak-in-Valencia.jpg)\n\nGrilled ribeye at Asador Argentino Gordon 10\n\n### Interno Mediterraneo\n\nI generally don’t eat much Italian food outside of Italy, but we’d had a recommendation for pizza in Russafa and off we went in search of it. Unfortunately, the pizza joint in question (which shall remain nameless) was abysmal. But by that point we needed a dough fix – fast. We stumbled upon [Interno Mediterraneo](https://www.tripadvisor.com/Restaurant_Review-g187529-d15599722-Reviews-Interno_Mediterraneo_Pizza-Valencia_Province_of_Valencia_Valencian_Community.html) quite by accident, but it turned out to be exactly what we were looking for. Pillowy Neapolitan-style crusts with fabulous-quality toppings, and some lovely cocktails to wash it all down with. I particularly enjoyed the *jamón y trufa* pizza with melt-in-the-mouth ham and a burrata-truffle cream sauce as well as fresh tomatoes. Plus, the smoky negroni made a great aperitivo.\n\n## Cafés in Valencia\n\nIn need of a restorative coffee fix or a refreshing beer? The coffee in Valencia seems generally to be of a high quality – I tend to order a *cortado* for a shot of espresso with a shot of warm milk to take the edge off. There’s also been a rise in Valencian craft beer since I started visiting: no longer are you limited to the local Turia lager – now you can find hoppy IPAs, sweet stouts and fruity white beers.\n\n### Café Artysana\n\nIf you find yourself in Russafa during the afternoon, looking for either a caffeine fix or somewhere to work on your laptop for an hour, pop into [Café Artysana](https://www.tripadvisor.com/Restaurant_Review-g187529-d8259851-Reviews-Cafe_ArtySana_Ruzafa-Valencia_Province_of_Valencia_Valencian_Community.html). Good coffee, tasty cakes, and chilled atmosphere to read, chat, or catch up on your emails. I’ve heard they also do brunch…\n\n### La Fábrica de Hielo\n\nIf you’re in El Cabanyal by the coast (for paella, perhaps), it’s definitely worth popping into cultural centre and hipster hangout [La Fábrica de Hielo](https://www.lafabricadehielo.net/) – literally translating as the ice factory. When I was there, the beer taps were flowing (with local beer Turia, among others), there was various street food on offer, and all this was accompanied by live music from a talented local duo singing and strumming.\n\n![Valencia bars - La Fabrica de Hielo](https://www.amsterdamfoodie.nl/wp-content/uploads/2018/11/Valencia-bars-La-Fabrica-de-Hielo-500x718.jpg)\n\nHipster heaven La Fabrica de Hielo\n\n### Ubik Café\n\nWe headed to [Ubik Café](http://ubikcafe.blogspot.com/) primarily because we’d read that it was dog-friendly, and once we arrived we discovered it was everyone-friendly. Kids, couples, friends, families and their furry companions are all welcome at this artsy Russafa café that’s filled with bustle and books.\n\n### If wine is as important as food on your trip, don’t miss my [Wine Lover’s Guide to Valencia](https://www.amsterdamfoodie.nl/food-travel/valencia-wine/). No seafood, no chorizo. The yellow rice heaped with prawns on menus is called **paella de marisco**, a separate dish, and delicious in its own right, just not the original Valencian dish.\n\nYou can find paella at just about any hour in most restaurants, and most will cook one for you there and then. Traditionally, though, it’s a **lunch dish**, eaten slowly in the early afternoon, and a few of the old-school spots still like a day’s notice for the real thing. If you’ve set your heart on a particular restaurant, a quick call ahead never hurts.\n\n**Must-try Valencian food and drink**\n\n* **Paella valenciana** – the original, chicken, rabbit and beans over rice\n* **Arròs del senyoret** – peeled-seafood rice, no shells to fight\n* **Fideuà** – paella’s noodle cousin, from the coast\n* **Esgarraet** – roasted red pepper and salt-cod salad\n* **Clóchinas** – small local wild mussels, summer only\n* **All i pebre** – eel and potato stew from the Albufera\n* **Esmorzaret** – the big mid-morning sandwich, with a drink\n* **Horchata and fartons** – tiger-nut drink with soft pastry fingers for dipping\n* **Agua de Valencia** – cava, orange juice, gin and vodka, by the jug\n* **Cremaet** – Valencian coffee laced with rum and spices\n* **Bunyols de carabassa** – pumpkin fritters shaped like doughnuts, the taste of Las Fallas in March\n\n## Where to eat paella\n\nPaella is one thing you have to try, but Valencia’s rice runs wider than that. Order **arròs del senyoret** and the seafood comes already peeled, so there’s no shelling at the table, or **arròs a banda**, cooked in a rich fish stock and served as just the rice. The best-known paella restaurants line the **Malvarrosa and Las Arenas seafront**, about 15 minutes from the old town by tram or metro, so leave time to get out there and back.\n\n**[La Pepica](https://lapepica.com/)** is where we ate our first paella, on the Malvarrosa seafront and serving since 1898. There’s real theatre to the arrival, a waiter bent under a pan so wide it takes over half the table. A lovely, easy introduction to Valencian rice, right by the water. We had a lovely evening here.\n\n**[Casa Carmela](https://www.casa-carmela.com/es/en/home/)**, a few minutes along the beach, is where our Airbnb host Carmen, a Valencia local, sent us for the wood-fired version – and it was amazing. Open since 1922, it cooks its rice over orange wood for a smoky edge and a crisp **socarrat** at the base of the pan. It’s reservations only and fills fast, so book.\n\n[**La Marcelina**](https://www.facebook.com/resturantelamarcelina/) is the other Malvarrosa classic, on the Paseo de Neptuno with tables looking straight out to sea. Its set arrocería menu is good value, and you choose your rice, from the traditional valenciana to its own seafood ‘Marcelina’. Book a table on the terrace.\n\n[**La Riuà**](https://www.lariua.com/en) is the old-town choice if you’d rather not head to the coast. A tiled, family-run classic in the historic centre, it has been serving rice and seafood for decades and does a fine traditional paella.\n\n[**Casa Roberto**](https://casaroberto.es/) is the one to head for in the city centre, a long-running, old-school arrocería where locals book in for a rice lunch. The arròs del senyoret, all peeled seafood, is what to order.\n\n## The best tapas in Valencia\n\n[**Sabbia**](https://sabbiavalencia.com/en/) was our first long lunch, a bright, open, contemporary space right on Las Arenas beach. The signature patatas bravas were the standout, served not as the usual pile but as individual small potatoes each filled with aioli, and the octopus tacos were sublime. Modern Mediterranean cooking a few steps from the sand, and in the evenings it turns into more of a scene, with live music.\n\n[**Casa Montaña**](https://www.emilianobodega.com/) was the best meal we had, and it isn’t paella. This bodega in the **El Cabanyal neighbourhood**, the old fishermen’s quarter by the beach, has been going since 1836. Giant oak barrels line the walls, and the wine list runs hundreds deep. It gets packed, so we booked a table ahead, which I’d strongly recommend, then settled in and kept ordering: clóchinas, anchovies, croquettes and more wine.\n\n[**Central Bar**](https://m.centralbar.es/), inside the Mercado Central (Mercat Central in Valencian), is the no-bookings counter from two-Michelin-starred chef Ricard Camarena. Order the cuttlefish or the house bocadillo, stand at the marble and watch the market move around you. It’s some of the best value in the city, and a fine spot for **jamón ibérico** too.\n\n[**Tasca Ángel**](https://www.facebook.com/victorarenasvergara#) is a standing-room tapas bar behind La Lonja, going since 1946 and loud with regulars. The fried sardines are the house dish, with snails, grilled artichokes and all i pebre alongside. Squeeze in if you can.\n\n[**La Pilareta**](https://www.barlapilareta.es/en) is one of the oldest tapas bars in the old town, near the Torres de Quart. Come for the **clóchinas** in summer and the famous calamares, among barrels of vermouth and a floor made for dropping your toothpicks on.\n\n## Brunch and breakfast\n\n[**Boa Beach**](https://www.instagram.com/boabeachvalencia/?hl=en) was our breakfast favourite, right on the sand near where we stayed. Great menu, a warm welcome, and poached eggs I still think about. Come for a slow morning before the beach fills up.\n\n[**Bodega La Pascuala**](https://bodegalapascuala.es/), another of Carmen’s steers, is the more local morning option in the beach neighbourhood. It’s the kind of place that’s full of regulars by 10am, good for a strong coffee, esmorzaret and something simple.\n\n[**Federal Café**](https://www.federalcafe.es/federalvalencia) in La Xerea does an Australian-style brunch, strong coffee, good pastries and a menu built for a lazy weekend morning, in a bright, busy room in the old town.\n\n[**Voltereta**](https://www.volteretarestaurante.com/en/restaurants/valencia/casa), a local favourite with leafy, tropical decor, is the trendier brunch pick, popular enough that you’ll want to go early or book. Good for smoothie bowls and brunch plates with a crowd.\n\n## The best bars and rooftops in Valencia\n\n[**Àtic at Palau Alameda**](https://palaualameda.com/atic) was one of our smarter nights out, a panoramic rooftop across the river with sunset views and a contemporary, brasserie feel. Go for the terrace and make sure you order some cocktails – they went down well!\n\n[**Café de las Horas**](https://www.cafedelashoras.com/) in the old town is the place to try an **Agua de Valencia**. It’s the city’s own cocktail, a mix of cava, fresh orange juice, gin and vodka, served by the jug and far more potent than it tastes. The room helps, all chandeliers and velvet, opulent and over the top, but in a good way.\n\n[**Café Madrid**](https://myrhotels.com/coctelerias/cafe-madrid/) is where the Agua de Valencia was invented, back in 1959, and it still makes a good one, either downstairs in the old café or up on its small rooftop. A lovely slice of the city’s drinking history.\n\nFor a sunset drink with a view, the rooftop bar at the [**MYR Palacio Vallier**](https://myrhotels.com/coctelerias/lladro-lounge-bar/) looks straight over the Plaza de la Virgen and the cathedral domes. Smart cocktails, open to non-guests, best an hour before the light goes.\n\n## Do try horchata\n\nValencia’s own drink is **horchata** (orxata), cold, sweet and milky, made from tiger nuts grown just north of the city. You dip **fartons** into it, long, soft, glazed pastry fingers made for exactly that, which soak up the horchata as you go. Skip the supermarket cartons entirely. It doesn’t sound very appetising, I grant you, but it’s really delicious and cooling.\n\n[**Horchatería Santa Catalina**](https://www.horchateriasantacatalina.com/en/) in the old town is the tiled, two-century-old classic, busy and often a queue – but worth the wait.\n\n[**Horchatería Daniel**](https://horchateria-daniel.es/) started out in the tiger-nut fields of Alboraya and has a counter in the Mercado de Colón, a good central spot for the real thing.\n\n[**Horchatería El Siglo**](https://elsiglorestaurante.com/), on the same square as Santa Catalina, is the quieter local alternative when the classic is heaving.\n\n## For a special meal\n\nFor a **romantic dinner**, a milestone **date night** or other **special occasions**, the city is in strong form. Valencia has **eight Michelin-starred restaurants**, with several more carrying **Repsol Suns**, Spain’s other major rating.\n\n[**Ricard Camarena**](https://ricardcamarena.com/) is the city’s best-known table, with **two Michelin stars and three Repsol Suns** for a vegetable-led tasting menu built on local produce, much of it from his own garden, in a converted factory. Book well ahead and go at lunch if you want to be kinder to your wallet.\n\n[**Flores Raras**](https://floresraras.com/) is Quique Dacosta’s fine-dining room near the town hall, with **two Michelin stars and two Repsol Suns**, which you may know as El Poblet until it was renamed earlier this year. Creative Valencian cooking for a seriously special occasion.\n\nBelow them sit the city’s one-star rooms. [**Fierro**](https://fierrovlc.com/) in Ruzafa seats barely a dozen and books out weeks ahead, while nearby [**La Salita**](https://www.visitvalencia.com/en/what-to-do-valencia/gastronomy/where-to-eat-restaurant-valencia/salita), chef Begoña Rodrigo’s one-star restaurant, is known for a creative, vegetable-led tasting menu. For Quique Dacosta’s cooking at a gentler price, [**Llisa Negra**](https://llisanegra.com/) does open-fire meat and rice in the centre with no tasting-menu commitment.\n\n## Other places worth a visit\n\nWe didn’t make it everywhere. However, here are a few more recommendations the guides and locals kept steering us towards, and worth booking a table if you’re staying for longer than a weekend – or on a return visit.\n\n[**Rausell**](https://www.rausell.es/), over in Arrancapins, is where Valencian chefs eat on their day off, a family-run seafood bar open since the 1940s, with fresh fish straight from the market, pristine sepionet, cigalas and some of the best patatas bravas in the city.\n\n[**Canalla Bistro**](https://www.canallabistro.com/en/home/) in Ruzafa is Ricard Camarena’s casual dining spot, playful and fun, with global small plates at a fraction of his tasting-menu price. A good way to eat his cooking without the occasion.\n\n[**Lambrusquería**](https://lambrusqueria.com/) on Conde Altea is the local shout for a night off from rice, a popular Italian in Eixample where you’ll need to book or turn up early.\n\n[**El Timbre**](https://www.facebook.com/people/El-Timbre/100054284630648/#) on Plaza Cánovas is a busy local tapas bar in El Pla del Remei, always full, with a terrace and a short menu of grilled plates, the octopus and the chistorra among them.\n\n## Food tours and paella classes\n\nThis is the home of paella, so learning to cook it is one of the better ways to spend a half-day here. The classic format is a **market-and-cook class**, where a chef walks you round the Mercado Central to buy the ingredients, then talks you through your own pan, usually finishing with tapas and wine. [**Sea Saffron**](https://getyourguide.tpk.lv/9efVobki) runs one of the best-rated, at around four hours.\n\nIf you’d rather eat than cook, a **[tapas and wine tour](https://getyourguide.tpk.lv/10mGiSHb)** or a morning market walk is the easiest way to taste a lot at once, the esmorzaret included. Most book through the usual platforms, so check recent reviews before you pick one.\n\n## Eating well on a budget\n\nYou don’t need a big budget to eat well here. At lunch, look for the **menú del día**, a set three-course meal with a drink that locals rely on, usually **€14 to €16**. It’s the most affordable way into home-style Valencian cooking, and the neighbourhood restaurants away from the tourist strips have the best prices.\n\nOnce you’ve eaten your way round, my [things to do in Valencia](https://www.my-travelmonkey.com/things-to-do-in-valencia/) and [where to stay in Valencia](https://www.my-travelmonkey.com/where-to-stay-in-valencia/) guides cover the rest, and the [3-day Valencia itinerary](https://www.my-travelmonkey.com/perfect-3-day-valencia-itinerary/) works the best of these meals into a plan.\n\n## Best restaurants in Valencia FAQs\n\n### What are the best restaurants in Valencia?\n\nFor traditional paella, La Pepica and Casa Carmela on the beach. For tapas, Casa Montaña in El Cabanyal. For a special meal, the two-Michelin-starred Ricard Camarena or Flores Raras.\n\n### Where are the best tapas in Valencia?\n\nCasa Montaña in El Cabanyal is the standout, a bodega going since 1836. In the old town, Central Bar inside the Mercado Central, Tasca Ángel and La Pilareta are all worth squeezing into.\n\n### What are the best bars in Valencia?\n\nCafé de las Horas for an Agua de Valencia, Café Madrid where the cocktail was invented in 1959, and the rooftops at Àtic and the MYR Palacio Vallier for a sunset drink.\n\n### What is in authentic paella valenciana?\n\nChicken, rabbit, flat green beans, butter beans, saffron, olive oil and bomba rice, with snails when in season. It contains no seafood and no chorizo.\n\n### Is seafood paella not authentic?\n\nSeafood paella can be ordered everywhere and is a popular dish, called paella de marisco. It’s just a separate thing from the original paella valenciana, which is made with meat.\n\n### When do locals eat paella?\n\nTraditionally at lunch, in the early afternoon, often on a Sunday. That said, you’ll find it served all day in the tourist-facing restaurants, and most will cook one to order on the spot.\n\n### What time do Valencians eat?\n\nLater than you might expect. Breakfast is light, then many stop for **almuerzo** (the esmorzaret, a mid-morning sandwich and a drink) around 10.30am. Lunch, the main meal, runs from about **2pm to 3.30pm**, and dinner is late, with kitchens often not opening until **8.30 or 9pm**. Afternoons are for horchata.\n\n### What is a farton?\n\nA farton is a long, soft, lightly glazed sweet pastry made to be dipped into horchata. It soaks up the drink and softens as you eat, and the two are almost always served together.\n\n### Where do locals eat in Valencia?\n\nThe old bodegas of El Cabanyal, the counters inside the Mercado Central, and the restaurants and Mercado de Ruzafa stalls over in Ruzafa. For everyday value, locals go where the menú del día is good.\n\n## Other Valencia articles\n\n##### [Best things to do in Valencia](https://www.my-travelmonkey.com/things-to-do-in-valencia/) [Where to stay in Valencia](https://www.my-travelmonkey.com/where-to-stay-in-valencia/) [Perfect 3-day Valencia itinerary](https://www.my-travelmonkey.com/perfect-3-day-valencia-itinerary/)\n\n### Leave a Reply [Cancel reply](/best-restaurants-in-valencia/#respond)\n\n#### Related Articles\n\n[Asia](https://www.my-travelmonkey.com/category/asia-2/) - Anywhere that’s busy with locals eating at 1:30 is going to be great – as the Spanish generally only go for lunch at 2pm – so you know they are going early to get a table and the most choice. Of course, busy places plan for multiple sittings but if you know a place is good we recommend going early to avoid waiting for a table. (It’s the only time we recommend eating before 2pm in Spain.)\n\n[![set-price meals in Spain](http://www.totallyspaintravel.com/wp-content/uploads/2016/02/MennudeldiaIMG_5062-1.jpg)](http://www.totallyspaintravel.com/wp-content/uploads/2016/02/MennudeldiaIMG_5062-1.jpg \"set-price meals in Spain\")\n\nWe usually steer clear of the pasta options on menus and we’d probably advise the same. But with a choice of three starters and mains, there’s always lots to choose from. First course: lentil stew, spaghetti bolognese, or, salad with crab sticks. Second course: chicken strips, Pork steak with veg, or, chorizo with egg. All for 9.80 euro\n\n**4 Where to Order a Menu del dia in Spain**\n\nSome of the chalk boards might look a bit rushed – and that’s a good sign – it has been written hastily by the chef after he or she has returned from the food market. This is why you rarely find translations of the menu in English. In fact, if you see anything that indicates that the actual menu wasn’t prepared that morning (laminated menus, translations or photos of what’s on the ‘menu’ that don’t look like they were done that morning) then avoid the place!\n\n[![set-price meals in Spain](http://www.totallyspaintravel.com/wp-content/uploads/2016/02/menudeldia11eurowicker-11.jpg)](http://www.totallyspaintravel.com/wp-content/uploads/2016/02/menudeldia11eurowicker-11.jpg \"set-price meals in Spain\")\n\nWe are over into the region of Asturias where beans and blue cheese are very popular! First course: bean stew, paella, Asturian pote (bean, potato & cabbage stew), seafood soup, or, mixed salad. Second course: roast lamb, wild boar with fries, breaded steaks with Cabrales blue cheese sauce, breaded steaks stuffed with ham & cheese, or, baked sea bream. With dessert, water, wine or cider for 11 euro\n\n**5 Why we’re so fond of the Menu del dia in Spain**\n\nOne of the reasons we love eating the ‘menu’ is because it is always local and seasonal. The chefs buy and serve what’s on offer at the market – which is what is in season. They also serve what the clients want to eat – which is warming and comforting stews in winter – with more grilled dishes in the Summer.\n\n[![set-price meals in Spain](http://www.totallyspaintravel.com/wp-content/uploads/2016/02/MenudelDiaredbrick14euroIMG_5063-11.jpg)](http://www.totallyspaintravel.com/wp-content/uploads/2016/02/MenudelDiaredbrick14euroIMG_5063-11.jpg \"set-price meals in Spain\")\n\nThis one is a bit more expensive than the average – you have to reckon the portions are even more generous or the quality of the wine is better. First course: salmon stew, veg stir-fry, or, salad with cured beef (cecina). Second course: pork tenderloin with cheese, fish of the day, or eggs with bacon. Plus dessert, drink and bread for 14 euro\n\n**6 The Drink(s) included in the Menu del Dia in Spain**\n\nIf you are eating on your own or with just one other person, you may have to decide between the red wine or the water or ask for both and be charged for the water. If there are four or you or more, you can just say ‘agua y vino’ and they’ll bring both to the table. In the larger cities, restaurants are starting to serve glasses of wine rather than bottles so don’t be insulted if it happens to you – just rejoice when it doesn’t. It’s unusual to have white wine with a set-price menu in Spain– but if that’s what you want, you’ll probably be brought a glass. If you don’t want wine and prefer a draught beer, ask for a cana. In the more rustic of places, when you order wine, you might be offered ‘Casera’ which is a fizzy soda water and is great for lifting what might be a fairly cheeky or young red wine. If you see others with a bottle of Casera on the table – you know what you are being given. Try it – it’s not bad.\n\n[![set-price meals in Spain](http://www.totallyspaintravel.com/wp-content/uploads/2016/02/MenuDelDiaredbrick15euroIMG_5064-low.jpg)](http://www.totallyspaintravel.com/wp-content/uploads/2016/02/MenuDelDiaredbrick15euroIMG_5064-low.jpg \"set-price meals in Spain\")\n\nAnother more upscale offering but this place is run by a well-known chef and the level of detail tells you this will be an elaborate meal. First course: mackerel tartar with tomato and avocado, vegetable stir-fry with saffron and jamon, or, chickpeas in vinaigrette with white anchovies. Second course: baked fillet of hake with ratatouille and garlic, slow cooked pork knuckle with cous cous, pine nuts and raisins, or, grilled shoulder of Iberian (acorn-fed) pork with rice, spinach, raisins & sweet and sour sauce. With bread, drink and dessert or coffee for 15 euro\n\n**7 Pan or Bread – Always included in the Menu del Dia in Spain**\n\nYou will always be served a basket of bread or a breadroll with or just before the first course appears. You may or may not be offered more bread if you finish the first batch. If you want more – ask for it. Butter is never served  – although if you specifically ask for it, it will be brought, with a raised eye-brow or two. You might find olive oil on the table and you can use that if you don’t like dry bread but we recommend you wait and dip the sauces from your plate. When in Spain!\n\n[![set-price meals in Spain](http://www.totallyspaintravel.com/wp-content/uploads/2016/02/MenudelDia15eurogreyIMG_5066-1.jpg)](http://www.totallyspaintravel.com/wp-content/uploads/2016/02/MenudelDia15eurogreyIMG_5066-1.jpg \"set-price meals in Spain\")\n\nThe writing looks a bit rushed but that probably means the chef wrote it. We’re being given very little detail about how the dishes are prepared which tells us this is probably a chef who delivers his dishes in a classic style. The interior also suggests the same – you can see one of those fridges at the door to showcase its raw materials. Again – here you are paying for simplicity and quality – we’d expect top-class service, top notch ingredients and a very handsome wine here. First course: Russian salad, Ratatouille, Red bean stew, or, Castilian Bread soup. Second course: Hake cheeks, fried sea bream, pork cheeks, or, breaded beefsteak with cheese. With dessert, bread, and a drink for 15 euro\n\n**8 Primero – First course of the Menu del Dia**\n\nThe first course isn’t an appetizer or starter – it’s actually your main course. So if you are hungry, this is where you need to pack in the carbs and the volume. Pulses are your friend if you are starving for two reasons – firstly because they are filling and secondly before they are often served in a big casserole pot from which you serve yourself – so you can have seconds and thirds – if you need to. Pastas and rice dishes also crop up here a lot. For those of you looking to shed pounds rather than gain them – look out for the soups and the salads.\n\n[![set-price meals in Spain](http://www.totallyspaintravel.com/wp-content/uploads/2016/02/MenuDelDiapaseopereda14.90IMG_5087-11.jpg)](http://www.totallyspaintravel.com/wp-content/uploads/2016/02/MenuDelDiapaseopereda14.90IMG_5087-11.jpg \"set-price meals in Spain\")\n\nOn one of Santander’s poshest streets and you can still find places offering the ‘menu del dia’. While it’s not cheap, this includes coffee so it’s almost a four-course menu. First course: Mixed salad served with cured meats and olives, paella, scrambled eggs, or, red bean stew. Second course: Baked steak au gratin, chicken breast with caramelised onions, or, baked hake with a stuffing. With a drink, bread, dessert AND a coffee for 14.90 euro\n\n**9 Segundo – Second course of the Menu del Dia**\n\nThe second course is where you get your protein. You’ll find a meat, fish and perhaps an egg-based dish here. The pricier the menu – the better the cuts of meat or varieties of fish. But cheaper cuts don’t mean bad quality – the opposite in fact. we love slow-cooked beef cheeks, the homemade meatballs in a rich sauce and the stuffed peppers – these are the dishes you rarely prepare at home. Sometimes these are served or garnished with a few fried potatoes or side-salad but you’ll find plenty of places that just serve it as it is. This is how the Spanish eat.\n\n[![set-price meals in Spain](http://www.totallyspaintravel.com/wp-content/uploads/2016/02/MenudelDiaboard10euroIMG_5102-1.jpg)](http://www.totallyspaintravel.com/wp-content/uploads/2016/02/MenudelDiaboard10euroIMG_5102-1.jpg \"set-price meals in Spain\")\n\nWe like when it’s obvious that the board is written up every day. You can see this one is weed clean every day as the writing wouldn’t be so fresh if it was there for a few days. First course: Paella, Beef and potato stew, salad with cured meats, or, pasta with a seafood sauce. Second course: chicken thighs in curry, lamb cutlets, scrambled eggs with fries and gulas (eel-like seafood strips), or, grilled salmon with salad. With bread, a drink and dessert for 10 euro. (11.50 on the terrace)\n\n**10 Postre – Dessert on the Menu del Dia**\n\nDesserts tend to be egg-based in Spain. The king of all menu del dia desserts is the ‘flan’ which is a crème-caramel often served with cream. Rice pudding is another one that crops up a lot. And natillas which is a custard. You might also be offered ice-cream which is probably not home-made. It’s often a Magnum or Cornetto. Fresh fruit is another popular offering. And yoghurt is another. Even if you aren’t offered fruit – you can ask whether they have any. We love when strawberries are in full season around Easter time and the restaurants prepare them with cream, orange juice or our old favourite balsamic vinegar.\n\n[![set-price meals in Spain](http://www.totallyspaintravel.com/wp-content/uploads/2016/02/MenuDelDia9euroIMG_5105.jpg)](http://www.totallyspaintravel.com/wp-content/uploads/2016/02/MenuDelDia9euroIMG_5105.jpg \"set-price meals in Spain\")\n\nOne of our favourites and not just because of the price. The food and the service is great! It’s unusual to see what’s on offer for dessert – probably because there’s no room on the chalkboard. First course: Cantabrian bean stew, seafood rice dish, spaghetti with prawns, mixed salad, or, cream of vegetable soup. Second course: slow cooked beef shank, stuffed chicken breast, beef steak, battered anchovies, or grilled sardines. With bread, a drink, a mystery dessert or coffee for 9 euro\n\n**11 Coffee…Instead of Dessert on the Menu del Dia**\n\nYou can order a coffee afterwards and it will be added to the bill as an extra. If you haven’t ordered dessert, you generally won’t be charged for the coffee (it replaces the dessert). When having your coffee, you might be offered a free digestif if you look like you’re not in a rush. ‘Chupito’ is the word for an after dinner snifter. Go with whatever is on offer. It’ll come in a shot glass and might be herbal, honey-infused or something sweet like a hazelnut, coffee or Bailey-like cream liqueur.\n\n[![set-price meals in Spain](http://www.totallyspaintravel.com/wp-content/uploads/2016/02/MenuDelDia12.50IMG_5106-1.jpg)](http://www.totallyspaintravel.com/wp-content/uploads/2016/02/MenuDelDia12.50IMG_5106-1.jpg \"set-price meals in Spain\")\n\nSometimes the board outlines the quantity of liquids on offer. Here we are told it’s half a bottle of wine (that’s three glasses!) per person. There are some countries where you pay 12.50 euro for the wine alone! First course: lentil stew with chorizo, vegetables au gratin with brie cheese, or, warm salad with wild mushrooms. Second course: lamb and potato stew, blue whiting in batter served with salad, or, beef cordon bleu (beef with ham and cheese served in batter). Dessert: pear tart, baked apple with caramel, or ice-cream. With bread, and a drink for 12.50 euro. (30 cent supplement on the terrace)\n\n**12 Cuenta – The Bill**\n\nSometimes the waiter will bring out the bill when you’ve ordered your coffee. If not just ask for “la cuenta cuando puedas”. The bill should be the exact price of the menu plus whatever coffees and other extra drinks you’ve ordered. You may be charged a terrace supplement – but this should have been pointed out to you already. Very occasionally you will have taxes added onto the price – although this is both illegal and annoying. Prices advertised to the public should always state VAT (known as IVA).\n\n[![set-price meals in Spain](http://www.totallyspaintravel.com/wp-content/uploads/2016/02/MenudelDiaSidreria20151129_160721-21.jpg)](http://www.totallyspaintravel.com/wp-content/uploads/2016/02/MenudelDiaSidreria20151129_160721-21.jpg \"set-price meals in Spain\")\n\nDefinitely worth a closer look this typical Asturian menu! First course: bean stew, beans with clams, beans with gulas (eel like seafood), red beans with rice, octopus and potato stew, sausage and potato stew, cod with chickpeas, lentil stew, meat paella, or, salad. Second course: venison stew, slow cooked pork knuckle with/without Cabrales blue cheese, stew pork cheek, breaded steak with eggs and chips, roast chicken drumsticks, meatballs with chips, baked cod, grilled seabass, or squid in its ink with fries. With dessert, a drink for 10 euro (8 euro for half menu)\n\n**13 Tipping after your Menu del dia in Spain**\n\n**Pro-tip: Although it’s never expected in Spain, we would leave a tip of 1 euro per person if you enjoyed your meal. If you adored it, we would leave 2 euro per person  – but that’s definitely not expected.** Read more about tipping in Spain [here](http://www.totallyspaintravel.com/2014/04/28/tipping-in-spain/).\n\n[![set-price meals in Spain](http://www.totallyspaintravel.com/wp-content/uploads/2016/02/1.MenudelDiamediomenuIMG_5104-1.jpg)](http://www.totallyspaintravel.com/wp-content/uploads/2016/02/1.MenudelDiamediomenuIMG_5104-1.jpg \"set-price meals in Spain\")\n\nThis place is pushing its two-course ‘menu’ for 6.50 which is usually the first course and dessert – perfect for anybody in a rush or if you want to enjoy dinner out in the evening. First course: Russian salad, scrambled eggs, or, green bean stew. Second course: tripe stew, pork steak with chips, fried anchovies. With bread, wine and dessert for 10 euro\n\n**14 Alternatives to the Men del Dia in Spain?**\n\nSome restaurants are aware that the two-hour lunchbreak doesn’t suit everybody. If you see the ½ menu, it usually refers to having a starter and a dessert for a little over half the price of a three course. Other premises offer a ‘plato del dia’ which might cost about 5 euro and you pay your drinks, bread etc as extra. There is always the plato combinado option which is usually a grilled steak with chips with a side salad or eggs with chips and chorizo. And of course – everywhere will have the ‘a la carte’ menu as well. Plus the degustacion or tasting menu – although this is usually at weekends and evenings.\n\n[![set-price meals in Spain](http://www.totallyspaintravel.com/wp-content/uploads/2016/02/MenuDelDiaOutsideAsturias10euro20151129_124416-1.jpg)](http://www.totallyspaintravel.com/wp-content/uploads/2016/02/MenuDelDiaOutsideAsturias10euro20151129_124416-1.jpg \"set-price meals in Spain\")\n\nPasta dishes usually turn up on the first course but here you can see it’s the second course – probably because it’s bolognese which is a meaty sauce. First course: bean stew, stir fried peas with jamon, pasta salad, mixed salad, or, Cuban style rice (with tomato and garlic). Second course: breaded steaks with Cabrales blue cheese, Asturias style tripe (with red peppers and pork), spaghetti bolognese, pork loin steak, or, hake in batter. With wine or water, bread and a dessert for 10 euro\n\n[![set-price meals in Spain](http://www.totallyspaintravel.com/wp-content/uploads/2016/02/IMG_5145-1.jpg)](http://www.totallyspaintravel.com/wp-content/uploads/2016/02/IMG_5145-1.jpg \"set-price meals in Spain\")\n\nAnd to sign off – one with a Catalan leaning! First course: Calcots (a scallion-like veg) with Romesco (red pepper and almond) sauce, mixed salad, stir-fried artichokes, pork knuckle soup, or, goats cheese salad. Second course: roast chicken, grilled salmon, flame grilled pork belly, flame grilled sausage, flame grilled steak (3 euro supplement), or flame grilled lamb (3 euro supplement). With dessert of creme caramel, fruit, ice-cream or pastry, plus bread, wine or water for 9.50 euro (half menu for 6.25 euro)\n\nSo that’s the basics about the menu del dia in Spain. Want to read more
